Nagoya Castle at a glance

Nagoya Castle sits on the north side of central Nagoya. In 2026 it is still one of the main sightseeing stops in the city, even though its famous main keep cannot be entered. This guide covers only the practical details: what you can see now, what a ticket costs since the October 2026 change, when to arrive so you don't miss last entry, and how to get there. If you want ideas for building a full day around Nagoya, see Day trips from Nagoya.

Key points at a glance (all checked against official pages on 26 November 2026):

  • Hours: 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The castle entrance closes at 4:00 p.m. (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/information/)
  • Last entry to Hommaru Palace and Nishinomaru Okura Museum: 4:00 p.m. (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/)
  • Closed: 29 December to 1 January. The Japanese page notes this may change because of events. (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/)
  • Adult admission: 1,000 yen since 1 October 2026 (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/)
  • Junior high school students and younger: free (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/)
  • Main keep: cannot be entered (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/)
  • Nearest subway station: Nagoyajo (M07) on the Meijo Line, about 5 minutes on foot from Exit 7 (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/access/)

Is the main keep (tenshu) open?

No. The official English homepage says the castle is open, but that the tower keep and some other structures cannot be entered (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/). According to the official Keep Reconstruction Project page, the keeps were closed because of aging and earthquake-resistance problems, and Nagoya City plans to rebuild them (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/tenshu/).

None of the official pages checked for this guide on 27 November 2026 gives a reopening or completion date for the tower keep. If a date matters for your trip, check the official reconstruction page close to when you travel.

The keep closure doesn't mean the castle grounds are closed. The official Japanese FAQ says visitors can still enter the grounds, including Hommaru Palace, the important cultural properties, the gardens, the stone walls and the seasonal flowers (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/faq/). The official English map still shows the Main Tower Keep and Sub Tower Keep in the Hommaru area, so you can see them from outside even though you can't go in (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/map/).

Opening hours, last entry and closed days

Opening hours are 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/information/). The official English visitor page also says the castle entrance closes at 4:00 p.m., so 4:00 p.m. is effectively the latest time to arrive (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/information/).

There's one more catch for late-afternoon visitors. Last admission to Hommaru Palace and to the Nishinomaru Okura Museum is also 4:00 p.m. (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/). It takes about 10 minutes to walk from the Main Gate or the East Gate to Hommaru Palace (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/information/). The official pages advise heading straight to the palace entrance if you're arriving late (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/). If you come through the gate at 3:55 p.m., you'll probably miss the palace.

Closed days: 29, 30 and 31 December and 1 January, four days in total. The Japanese information page adds that closed days may change because of events (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/). There's no regular weekly closing day on the official pages checked for this guide.

Tickets and admission fees from 1 October 2026

Admission went up on 1 October 2026. The official Japanese fee page carries an important notice saying the fees changed from that date (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/). The official Japanese FAQ explains that the adult fee was 500 yen before the change and became 1,000 yen from 1 October 2026. Junior high school students and younger stay free (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/faq/). The FAQ also confirms that the keep closure doesn't reduce the fee.

Current individual and group fees, as printed on the official fee page (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/):

Category Individual Group of 30+ Group of 100+
Adult 1,000 yen 900 yen 800 yen
Nagoya City residents aged 65+ 300 yen 270 yen 240 yen
Junior high school students and younger Free Free Free

The English visitor page shows the same figures (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/information/). To get the senior rate you need proof of Nagoya residence, age and identity, such as photo ID (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/). The senior rate only applies to Nagoya City residents, so most overseas and out-of-town visitors pay the adult fee.

Other tickets on the official fee page:

  • Tokugawaen joint ticket: adult 1,040 yen, Nagoya City 65+ 320 yen, cash only, usable on any date (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/)
  • Annual pass (1 year): adult 4,000 yen, Nagoya City 65+ 1,300 yen (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/). The FAQ says the pass is valid for one year from the first entry and needs a photo attached (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/faq/).

The Japanese FAQ says joint tickets and annual passes are sold at the Main Gate and East Gate ticket windows (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/faq/).

Sightseeing-bus pass discount: The official Nagoya tourism page for the Me-guru sightseeing bus lists One-Day Pass discounts at facilities on the route, including castle admission of 1,000 yen down to 800 yen for adults aged 18 and older, and 500 yen down to 400 yen for junior-high and high-school students and seniors aged 65 and older (Me-guru). The same page says you need the One-Day Pass to receive those benefits. Confirm the current conditions and any ticket-window procedure on that page before you go.

How to pay and where to buy tickets

According to the official fee page, the castle ticket counter takes cash (yen) and cashless payment, including credit cards, transit and other e-money cards, and QR-code payments (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/). The Tokugawaen joint ticket is the exception: it's cash only (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/).

The official fee page also links to web tickets sold through an external booking site (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/fee/). Buying online doesn't change the 4:00 p.m. entrance and palace cut-off.

Getting to Nagoya Castle from Nagoya Station

Most visitors start at Nagoya Station. Airport arrivals can see Chubu Centrair Airport to Nagoya first. The official castle pages list station and stop access but don't print a route from Nagoya Station (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/access/). These are the options you can piece together from official pages:

Option 1: Me-guru sightseeing bus (direct). The official Nagoya tourism page lists the Me-guru route with stop 0 at Nagoya Station and stop 4 at Nagoya Castle. Stop 5 is Nagoya Castle East/Shiyakusho (City Hall) (https://www.nagoya-info.jp/en/useful/meguru/). The castle's official access page also lists Me-guru (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/access/). Operating days, timetable and fare are on the Me-guru pages; this guide does not copy a full timetable. Check the current timetable before you board, because some departures skip Nagoya Castle. Some departures skip Nagoya Castle, so check the current timetable before you board (https://www.nagoya-info.jp/en/useful/meguru/).

Option 2: Subway to Nagoyajo (M07). The nearest station is Nagoyajo on the Meijo Line. It's about 5 minutes on foot from Exit 7 (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/access/). The castle pages don't give the route from Nagoya Station to Nagoyajo, any transfers, the journey time or the fare. The castle site and the Transportation Bureau homepage do not print a single Nagoya Station–Nagoyajo subway fare in the pages checked for this guide (Transportation Bureau). Use the Bureau’s own route and fare search for the current figure.

Option 3: City bus. The official access pages list City Bus route Sakae 13 to the Nagoyajo Seimon-mae stop, and Key Route Bus No. 2 to Shiyakusho (City Hall), about 5 minutes on foot (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/access/). Departure points from Nagoya Station and the bus fares are not printed on the castle access page checked for this guide. Use the Transportation Bureau’s current information.

Other ways to arrive: subway, Meitetsu, bus, SRT, cruise and car

From the official access pages (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/access/ and https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/information/):

  • Tsurumai Line: Sengencho (T05) is listed as an access station. The official access page checked for this guide does not print a walking time from that station.
  • Meitetsu Seto Line: Higashi-Ote, about 15 minutes on foot.
  • SRT: the "Nagoya Castle" stop, about 4 minutes on foot. The Japanese access page calls this a temporary stop until around spring 2027.
  • Nagoya Horikawa Cruise: get off at the Nagoyajo-mae (Asahibashi) landing, about 5 minutes on foot.
  • By car: about 8 minutes south of the Kurokawa exit on Nagoya Expressway Route 1 (Kusunoki Line), or about 5 minutes north of the Marunouchi exit on the City Loop.

Parking. The official pages list two castle-area car parks. Both charge 250 yen per 30 minutes for cars and 150 yen per 30 minutes for motorcycles (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/access/):

  • Main Gate (Seimon-mae) car park: 8:45 a.m. to 9:30 p.m.
  • Ninomaru East (East Gate) car park: 8:30 a.m. to 10:30 p.m.

The English page says overnight parking isn't allowed at the castle car parks (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/information/). The English and Japanese pages print different capacity figures, so this guide leaves capacity out.

Inside the grounds: Hommaru Palace and Nishinomaru Okura Museum

With the keep closed, Hommaru Palace is the main indoor highlight. The official English page describes extensive use of hinoki cypress, with metal fittings and wall paintings recreated by traditional craftspeople (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/nagoyajo/honmarugoten/). Entry is included with a Nagoya Castle ticket (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/nagoyajo/honmarugoten/).

Palace rules printed on the official page (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/nagoyajo/honmarugoten/):

  • Shoes come off at the entrance. Slippers are provided for visitors with bare feet or stockings.
  • Suitcases and large bags go in free lockers inside the entrance.
  • No flash photography.
  • No food, drink or smoking, and the page restricts mobile phone use.
  • Don't touch sliding doors, paper screens or metal fittings.
  • The palace has no air conditioning and no toilets, and the lighting is kept low.
  • Wheelchair access is on the north side, and special wheelchairs for the interior are available.

The Nishinomaru Okura Museum is about 5 minutes on foot from the Main Gate and about 10 minutes from the East Gate. Last entry is 4:00 p.m. (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/information/).

The official English map also lists the Ofuke-maru Exhibit Hall and tea ceremony houses in the Ofuke-maru area (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/en/map/).

Lockers, luggage, wheelchairs and pets

From the official Japanese FAQ (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/faq/):

  • Coin lockers are at the Main Gate and the East Gate.
  • Oversized baggage that won't fit in a locker can be left at the General Information Office at the Main Gate for 300 yen per item.
  • Wheelchairs are available at both gates. Ask at the ticket gate window. There are only a few, they can't be reserved, and you may have to wait.
  • Pets are allowed in the grounds on a leash, as long as they don't damage facilities or plants. Owners must clean up after them. Pets aren't allowed inside buildings such as Hommaru Palace and the Nishinomaru Okura Museum, except assistance dogs.

How long to spend and how to plan the visit

The official FAQ estimates about 1 to 1.5 hours to see the grounds including Hommaru Palace (https://www.nagoyajo.city.nagoya.jp/info/faq/). A simple plan based on the official times:

  1. Arrive well before 4:00 p.m. That's when the castle entrance closes, and it's also the last entry for the palace and museum.
  2. Decide which gate to use. Both have coin lockers and wheelchair loans. From either gate it's about 10 minutes on foot to Hommaru Palace.
  3. Go to Hommaru Palace first if you're short on time, then the museum, then the gardens and stone walls.
  4. Save the view of the keep exteriors for last. They can't be entered, but you can see them from the grounds.

Frequently asked questions

Can I go inside Nagoya Castle's main keep?
No. The official site says the tower keep and some other structures can't be entered. The pages checked on 27 November 2026 do not print a reopening date.
How much is Nagoya Castle admission now?
Since 1 October 2026, adults pay 1,000 yen. Junior high school students and younger are free. Nagoya City residents aged 65+ pay 300 yen with proof of eligibility.
What time is last entry?
The castle entrance closes at 4:00 p.m., and last entry to Hommaru Palace and the Nishinomaru Okura Museum is also 4:00 p.m. The grounds close at 4:30 p.m.
When is Nagoya Castle closed?
29 December to 1 January. The official site notes this may change because of events.
What is the nearest station?
Nagoyajo (M07) on the Meijo Line, about 5 minutes on foot from Exit 7. For the subway fare, use the Transportation Bureau’s official fare search; it is not printed on the castle pages checked for this guide.
