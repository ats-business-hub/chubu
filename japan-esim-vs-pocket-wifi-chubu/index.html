Japan eSIM vs pocket Wi-Fi for Chubu

A compatible phone can skip the counter. A router is easier to share. Compare before you fly.

For a Chubu trip — Nagoya, Takayama, Shirakawa-go, Kanazawa, or a later Kamikochi season — you usually choose between a Japan eSIM and a pocket Wi‑Fi router. An eSIM needs an unlocked, eSIM-compatible phone, and there is no device to carry or return. A pocket Wi‑Fi can be shared among a group; you pick it up, return it, and keep it charged. What to check on an eSIM

  • Your phone is unlocked and listed as eSIM-compatible. If it is not, skip this option.
  • Which Japanese network the plan uses. One Klook Japan eSIM listing is branded as Softbank 5G / IIJ (Docomo) 4G LTE — read the live listing, because products change.
  • Whether hotspot or tethering is allowed if you also need a laptop or a second phone.
  • When validity starts (on first connection abroad, or on a fixed date) and what happens if you run out of data.

What to check on pocket Wi‑Fi

  • Pickup and return points that actually fit your flights. Chubu Centrair (NGO) is a common option for this loop; confirm the terminal, counter or locker, and opening hours on the booking page. Some products let you return at a different airport.
  • Whether “unlimited” is subject to a fair-usage slowdown, and whether a 3 GB/day or 10 GB/day plan drops speed after the cap.
  • Inclusive-day counting: picking up Thursday and returning Monday can be charged as five days.
  • The loss, damage, and accessory fees, and whether a power bank is included.

For a day in Takayama or Shirakawa-go, either option can work if the plan has nationwide coverage. I would not rely on a single screenshot of “unlimited” in a mountain valley — download offline maps, and keep the official bus and train pages saved.

Frequently asked questions

Is an eSIM or a pocket Wi-Fi better for a Chubu trip?
An eSIM suits a traveller with an unlocked, eSIM-capable phone who wants to be online on arrival. A pocket Wi-Fi router is easier to share in a group, or when someone’s phone cannot take an eSIM. Compare plan length, fair-use rules, and pickup points before you pay.
What should I check before buying a pocket Wi-Fi?
Confirm the pickup and return airport — including Chubu Centrair if that is your arrival — on the booking page, whether “unlimited” is subject to a fair-use slowdown, how days are counted (often inclusively), and the loss fee. One Klook Ninja Wi-Fi listing publishes a 40,000 yen device-loss fee.
