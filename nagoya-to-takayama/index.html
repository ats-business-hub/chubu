Nagoya to Takayama: how to get there

A direct limited express, or a reserved highway bus in 2 hours 40 minutes. Official pages first.

I created the paid guide mentioned here. Some links on this page are affiliate links for GetYourGuide, Klook and Viator; if you book through them I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Official operator pages remain the primary place to book. I do not recommend day-tripping Shirakawa-go from Kanazawa and back, and I do not treat the nationwide Japan Rail Pass as the default for this loop.

There are two official ways from Nagoya to Takayama: JR Central’s Limited Express Hida, and the reserved highway bus jointly run by Nohi Bus, Meitetsu Bus, and JR Tokai Bus. The train is usually the more predictable ride; the bus is cheaper on the official tariff. Recheck today’s times and seats before you pay. The nationwide Japan Rail Pass is not the default for this hop.

By train: Limited Express Hida

The Hida is a direct JR limited express from Nagoya Station to Takayama Station (some trains continue to Toyama). Look up the day’s departures, travel time, and fare on official JR Central pages or ticket machines — I am not copying a timetable here, because times change. From 25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027, the official Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass page says all seats on Hida trains will be reserved only; if you travel then, obtain a reserved-seat ticket before you board.

If you buy the official Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass (adult 19,800 yen for five consecutive days, as published on 22 November 2026), it covers JR limited expresses on this corridor. Reserved seats on JR limited expresses and the Hokuriku Shinkansen are free of charge up to six times, according to the official seller. Exchange and coverage rules are on touristpass.jp. It is not the nationwide Japan Rail Pass, and it does not include the Tokaido Shinkansen.

By highway bus (official fares)

Nohi’s official English “Takayama–Nagoya Line” page lists a required time of 2 hours 40 minutes. All express buses require a reservation (reserved seats only). Reservations open one month before the travel date (the same day of the previous month). Book on highwaybus.com, or phone the Nohi Bus Reservation Center (9:00am–5:00pm, TEL 0577-32-1688) or the Meitetsu Bus Reservation Center (9:00am–6:00pm, TEL 0570-08-0489). Nohi says unpaid online reservations are cancelled automatically if payment is not completed within three days (by 24:00 JST, counting the reservation day).

Adult one-way fares published on Nohi’s official English page, checked 22 November 2026. Units are Japanese yen. The figures in parentheses are the web discount when you reserve and pay by card on highwaybus.com. Recheck before you travel.

Toward Takayama, you may board at Meitetsu Bus Center or at JR Nagoya Station; Nohi marks both as boarding-only, and Takayama Bus Terminal as drop-off only. The Meitetsu Bus Center is convenient if you use the Meitetsu line; JR Nagoya Station is convenient if you arrive by Shinkansen. You cannot get off at JR Nagoya Station when travelling toward Takayama, and you cannot board at JR Nagoya Station when travelling toward Nagoya — check the boarding/drop-off marks on the official timetable.

Nohi’s published Nagoya-bound departures from Takayama Bus Terminal start at 6:30 and finish at 19:00; Takayama-bound departures from Meitetsu Bus Center start at 7:00 and finish at 20:30. Recheck the full table, including which buses stop at Gujo Hachiman I.C., on the official page. Buses have a bathroom and are non-smoking. Keep power banks with you in the cabin, not in the trunk. This highway bus is a different product from the Takayama–Shirakawa-go bus, and I do not treat the area pass as covering this Nagoya–Takayama highway service unless the official pass page says so for your date — the coverage text I checked lists the Shirakawa-go / Kanazawa and Takayama / Toyama bus corridors, not this line.

Which should you take?

  • Take the Hida if you want a clock you can plan around, reserved seats, and a ride that is not delayed by expressway traffic. Buy or reserve as soon as official sales allow, especially on Fridays, Sundays, and holiday eves.
  • Take the highway bus if the official 3,300–3,600 yen tariff fits your budget better than the train, and you can live with road delays. Reserve early; all seats are reserved.
  • If you already hold the official area pass, the Hida is usually the seat you have already paid for. Confirm reserved-seat rules for your dates, especially 25 December 2026–5 January 2027.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the highway bus, and what does it cost?
Nohi’s official English page lists a required time of 2 hours 40 minutes. Adult one-way fares are 3,600 yen, or 3,300 yen with the web discount when you reserve and pay by card on highwaybus.com. All seats are reserved. Recheck before you travel.
Should I take the Limited Express Hida instead?
Take the Hida if you want a more predictable timetable and a reserved train seat. Look up today’s times and fares on official JR Central pages. From 25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027, the official area-pass page says all Hida seats are reserved only.
Does the official area pass cover the Nagoya–Takayama highway bus?
Do not assume that it does. The coverage text checked on the official pass page lists the Shirakawa-go / Kanazawa and Takayama / Toyama bus corridors, not this highway line. The pass does cover JR limited expresses on the Nagoya–Takayama corridor. Check the official map for your date.
