Japan Rail Pass vs Central Japan regional passes

Nationwide JR Pass at 50,000 yen, or a 19,800-yen regional pass that includes the Shirakawa-go buses? Official prices first.

Most Central Japan trips — Nagoya, Takayama, Shirakawa-go, Kanazawa — do not need the nationwide Japan Rail Pass. The question is whether a regional pass beats paying leg by leg. Prices below are from the official pass pages, checked 25 November 2026. They are adult prices in Japanese yen.

One official Area Pass note is easy to miss: the Tokaido Shinkansen between Kyoto and Shin-Osaka is not a valid route. If you start in Osaka or Kyoto with only that pass, do not assume you can ride the bullet train to Nagoya for free. Use the conventional JR route on the official map, enter via Tsuruga and the Hokuriku Shinkansen, buy a separate Tokaido Shinkansen ticket, or take the reserved Nohi highway bus — which the Area Pass also does not cover. The same is true of the Nohi/Keio Tokyo (Shinjuku)–Takayama highway bus: it is not on the official Area Pass bus list, and it is not a JR train, so the nationwide JR Pass does not cover it either. See Osaka or Kyoto to Takayama and Tokyo to Takayama.

Why the nationwide JR Pass rarely pays off for this loop

  • Buses are the key legs. Takayama–Shirakawa-go and Shirakawa-go–Kanazawa are Nohi/Hokutetsu highway buses. The official JR Pass scope page covers JR Bus local lines only, and not JR express bus routes; Nohi and Hokutetsu are not JR companies. The Takayama–Hokuriku pass does include those buses.
  • Price gap. 50,000 yen for 7 days versus 19,800 yen for 5 days. The JR Pass makes sense mainly when the same week also includes long Shinkansen rides, such as Tokyo or Osaka and back, on Hikari or Kodama (Nozomi needs a special ticket for pass holders).
  • Leg prices, for your own sum: Nohi’s fare notice lists Takayama–Shirakawa-go at 2,900 yen and Takayama–Kanazawa at 4,400 yen from 1 December 2026; Kanazawa–Shirakawa-go is 2,900 yen from the same date. JR Central’s ticketless page lists the Hida reserved-seat limited-express charge Nagoya–Takayama at 2,730 yen in normal season (the basic fare is extra). Which to choose

  • Nagoya → Takayama → Shirakawa-go → Kanazawa (→ back via Tsuruga or on to Osaka/Kyoto) within 5 days: price the Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass first. Detail: Takayama–Hokuriku Area Pass.
  • Tokyo–Nagoya–Kyoto–Osaka plus the Chubu loop in one week: compare the 7-day JR Pass with the area pass plus separate Shinkansen tickets.
  • Matsumoto or the Alpine Route: the Alpine–Takayama–Matsumoto pass has a limited season; the 2026 usage period has ended. Check touristpass.jp for 2027 dates before you plan around it.
  • Only Nagoya–Takayama return: buy point-to-point, or take the highway bus. See Nagoya to Takayama.
  • Year-end: touristpass.jp says all Limited Express Hida seats (and Shinano) are reserved-only from 25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027; get your free seat reservation before boarding.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Japan Rail Pass worth it for Takayama and Shirakawa-go?
Usually not on its own. As of 25 November 2026 the 7-day ordinary JR Pass is 50,000 yen online, and it does not cover the Nohi or Hokutetsu highway buses to Shirakawa-go. The 5-day Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass is 19,800 yen and includes those buses.
Does the Japan Rail Pass cover the Limited Express Hida?
Yes. The official scope page says the pass covers JR limited express trains; the Hida is a JR train. You still need a seat reservation on reserved-only dates.
Can I still use the Alpine–Takayama–Matsumoto pass in winter?
No. Its 2026 usage period on the official page ran from 15 April to 10 November 2026. Check touristpass.jp for 2027 dates.
