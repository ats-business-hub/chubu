7 winter photo spots you can reach on a day trip from Nagoya

Nagoya sits in the middle of Japan's Chubu region, and in winter that puts snowy villages, castles, post towns and a mountain ropeway within day-trip range. This list gathers seven places where winter adds something to a photo: snow on thatched roofs, rime ice on a summit, a castle above morning cloud, or a town lit up after dark.

It's built to scan. Four of the spots already have full guides on this site, so they get a short summary and a link. The other three get a little more space, and Yunoyama Onsen and Mt. How to use this list

Winter days are short, and several of these places run shorter winter hours. Plan around the official closing or last-entry time first, then work backward to your departure from Nagoya.

A few habits make a winter photo day easier:

  • Check the official page the night before. Snow, wind and events can change operations, especially on mountain ropeways and at the Shirakawa-go light-up.

1. Shirakawa-go

Shirakawa-go's Ogimachi village is a registered World Heritage Site. It has more than 100 gassho-style buildings, large and small, and people still live in them (shirakawa-go.gr.jp). Under snow, the steep thatched roofs are the classic Chubu winter photo.

The 2027 Winter Light-Up is scheduled for January 11 (a public holiday) and the Sundays of January 17, 24 and 31 (shirakawa-go.gr.jp). The official site has posted notices on regular car parking reservations for the light-up and on a reservation system for the bus parking lot, and says additional light-up accommodation reservations are sold out (shirakawa-go.gr.jp). Read the official precautions page before you plan a light-up visit. For routes and timing, see Shirakawa-go day trip from Nagoya and Shirakawa-go winter light-up 2027. If you would rather join a tour, KKday lists a Shirakawa-go day tour from Nagoya (affiliate link, #ad; check what each listing includes).

2. Takayama old town

Takayama's Sanmachi Traditional Buildings Preservation Area is the old-town highlight that Hida's official tourism site features (hida.jp). Snow on dark wooden merchant houses makes for quiet, high-contrast frames, especially early in the day.

The official site also posts winter driving safety tips, worth reading if you plan to drive (hida.jp). For a winter walking guide, see Takayama in winter. For train options, see Nagoya to Takayama. To leave bags at the station, see Takayama Station luggage storage.

3. Nabana-no-sato illumination

Nabana-no-sato in Kuwana is known for its winter illumination, which its official site lists among the park's attractions alongside fireflies and day-use hot springs (nagashima-onsen.co.jp). It's the easiest after-dark photo trip on this list, and you can pair it with an afternoon somewhere else.

The official illumination page gives the 2026–27 season as 17 October 2026 to 31 May 2027, closed on 6 and 7 January 2027. From 15:00 to 23:00 on 23–25 December, entry needs a special date-specified ticket sold in advance at convenience stores (FamilyMart and 7-Eleven) and on Asoview!, and same-day tickets may not be available (nagashima-onsen.co.jp). Check that page for current hours and admission. For access from the city and photo tips, see Nabana no Sato illumination from Nagoya. To compare guided options around the city, see Nagoya tours and activities on KKday (affiliate link, #ad).

4. Matsumoto Castle

Matsumoto Castle is a National Treasure of Japan, as its official English site states (matsumoto-castle.jp). A black keep against snow and clear winter sky is one of the strongest castle photos in central Japan.

The official site says drones are prohibited (matsumoto-castle.jp). The Japanese visitor information page lists hours of 8:30–17:00 (last entry 16:30) and says the castle is open every day except 29–31 December. Adult admission is ¥1,200 with a date-and-time e-ticket or ¥1,300 for a same-day paper ticket; elementary and junior-high students pay ¥400 (matsumoto-castle.jp). KKday also sells a Matsumoto Castle admission e-ticket (affiliate link, #ad). See Matsumoto Castle and Nagoya to Matsumoto by train in winter.

5. Yunoyama Onsen and Mt. Gozaisho

This is the closest snow-and-mountain photo trip to Nagoya on the list. The Gozaisho Ropeway climbs Mt. Gozaisho (1,212 m) from the Yunoyama Onsen hot-spring area in Mie Prefecture (japan.travel). Japan's official tourism site describes the summit ski area as the slopes closest to Nagoya (japan.travel).

In winter, the main photo subject is rime ice (juhyo) on the summit trees. Both the ropeway's official site and Mie's official tourism site name it as a winter highlight (gozaisho.co.jp; kankomie.or.jp). Mie's English tourism page also mentions frost-covered trees and icefalls (visitmie-japan.travel). The ropeway is 2,161 m long with a 780 m height difference, and the cabins look down over the Suzuka Mountains (kankomie.or.jp).

Key winter details: - Winter ropeway hours run December 1 to March 31: first ascent 9:00, last ascent 16:00 (gozaisho.co.jp; gozaisho.co.jp). - The adult round-trip fare is ¥2,600 (junior high and up), and the child fare is ¥1,300 (age 4 to elementary school) (gozaisho.co.jp). - Japan's official tourism site gives this route from Nagoya: Kintetsu Limited Express to Kintetsu Yokkaichi, a local train to Kintetsu Yunoyama Onsen, a Mie Kotsu bus, then about a 10-minute walk to the ropeway. It puts the trip at about 1 hour 20 minutes (japan.travel). This guide does not quote the Kintetsu fare; look it up on Kintetsu's official fare search (ticket.kintetsu.co.jp).

At the foot of the mountain, Yunoyama Onsen has a reputation that the national tourism site traces back about a thousand years (japan.travel). Its riverside parks and rock gorges are good for slower photos after the summit.

For the full breakdown of fares, winter hours, the highway bus option and a sample day, see the dedicated guide: Yunoyama Onsen and Gozaisho Ropeway day trip.

6. Narai-juku

Narai-juku is a post town on the Nakasendo, the Edo-period highway between Edo and Kyoto. Its official site describes it as the midpoint of the route: the 34th station counting from either end, out of 67 (naraijuku.com). It sits at the highest elevation of the eleven Kiso post towns, near the Torii Pass, and was nicknamed "Narai Senken," roughly "Narai of a thousand houses" (naraijuku.com).

For photographers, the draw is length and continuity. Shiojiri's official tourism site describes about 1 km of townscape along the Narai River and calls it the longest post town in Japan (tokimeguri.jp). The streetscape was designated an Important Preservation District for Groups of Traditional Buildings in 1978 (naraijuku.com), and residents still live there (tokimeguri.jp). A thin layer of snow on the eaves and an empty street early in the day make the long receding lines of wooden facades stand out.

Getting there and around: - Narai-juku is a 3-minute walk from JR Narai Station (shoryudo.go-centraljapan.jp). This guide does not quote train times or fares from Nagoya; check them with JR Central (global.jr-central.co.jp) before you go. - By car, the official guide recommends the free parking at the Kiso no Ohashi roadside station or the paid Gonbee parking lot (tokimeguri.jp). The official site posted a notice on August 13, 2026 about repair work on the Kiso no Ohashi bridge, so check it before you go (naraijuku.com). - The Narai-juku Tourist Information Office and the Shiojiri City Tourist Information Center close from December 29 to January 3 (tokimeguri.jp).

The official site has an events section, but its home page did not list winter events when checked on 27 November 2026 (naraijuku.com). The official site gives the information center's phone number as 0264-34-3160 (naraijuku.com). For wider Nagano travel information, see the prefectural tourism site (go-nagano.net).

7. Gujo Hachiman

Gujo Hachiman is a water town in Gifu Prefecture with spring-fed channels, old merchant streets and a hilltop castle. Its official site puts it about one hour from Nagoya (gujohachiman.com). Winter brings three distinct photo subjects.

The castle in snow. The official site quotes the writer Shiba Ryotaro calling Gujo Hachiman Castle the most beautiful mountain castle in Japan, and it notes how the castle looks under snow (gujohachiman.com). The current wooden keep was rebuilt in 1933, modeled on Ogaki Castle, and the original stone walls remain (gujohachiman.com). From November to February the castle is open 9:00–16:30, with last entry 15 minutes before closing. Admission is ¥400 for adults and ¥200 for children (gujohachiman.com). The mountain road has sharp curves, and large vehicles or vehicles seating six or more cannot use it. You can park near the Yamauchi Kazutoyo and Chiyo statue and walk up in about 15–20 minutes (gujohachiman.com).

The "castle in the sky." From October to March, early mornings often show the castle rising above a sea of clouds as seen from Horikoshi Pass on National Route 256 (gujohachiman.com). The official site is firm about safety here: don't park on the road or set up tripods on it. It's a no-parking zone with blind curves, and roadside photographers have caused dangerous situations (gujohachiman.com). Use proper parking only.

Kanzarashi in the rivers. In winter, Gujo Honzome dyed cloth is rinsed in the cold river water near Sogi-sui, a spring listed among Japan's 100 Famous Waters (gujohachiman.com). The official page describes the rinsing as a tradition held around Daikan, the coldest point of the year, with the koi-nobori lit up on the river the evening before. Its listed dates and weekdays do not match each other, so this guide does not give a date; check the page or the tourism office before you plan around it (gujohachiman.com).

The Kitamachi district, about 14.1 hectares of merchant houses and water channels, is a designated preservation district for traditional buildings (gujohachiman.com). For rail, the official site promotes a one-day Gujo Hachiman coupon on the Nagaragawa Railway. It covers unlimited rides between Mino-Ota and Gujo-Hachiman plus admission to the castle and the Hakurankan museum, and it's sold as a mobile ticket. The page checked does not print the price (gujohachiman.com). For municipal notices, see Gujo City (city.gujo.gifu.jp).

Winter photo-day checklist

  • Anchor on official cutoffs. Gozaisho's last winter ascent (16:00) and the Gujo Hachiman Castle closing time (16:30) set the end of your day at those spots (gozaisho.co.jp; gujohachiman.com).
  • Respect photo rules. Matsumoto Castle prohibits drones (matsumoto-castle.jp), and Gujo bans roadside parking and tripods at the Horikoshi Pass viewpoint (gujohachiman.com).
  • Book light-up logistics early. Shirakawa-go's light-up uses parking reservations, and additional accommodation reservations are already sold out (shirakawa-go.gr.jp).
  • Protect your gear. When you come in from the cold, leave the camera in a closed bag for a while to limit condensation. Carry spare batteries, which drain faster in the cold.
  • Allow for weather. Mountain operations can stop for wind or snow. Gozaisho's site shows the summit status, and its price page links to a suspension notice page (gozaisho.co.jp; gozaisho.co.jp).
  • Stay connected. Compare a Japan SIM or eSIM from Sakura Mobile or Japan Wireless (affiliate links, #ad), or read Japan eSIM vs pocket Wi-Fi for Chubu.

Frequently asked questions

Which of these winter photo spots is closest to Nagoya?
Yunoyama Onsen and Mt. Gozaisho. Japan's official tourism site gives about 1 hour 20 minutes from Nagoya to the ropeway by Kintetsu train and bus (japan.travel). Gujo Hachiman's official site puts the town at about one hour from Nagoya (gujohachiman.com).
When is the Shirakawa-go Winter Light-Up in 2027?
January 11, 17, 24 and 31, 2027, according to the official site (shirakawa-go.gr.jp). Check its precautions and parking reservation notices before you travel.
Where can I photograph rime ice near Nagoya?
At the summit of Mt. Gozaisho, reached by the Gozaisho Ropeway. The official ropeway and Mie tourism sites both name rime ice as a winter highlight (gozaisho.co.jp; kankomie.or.jp). Conditions vary, so check the summit status on the ropeway site.
Can I use a drone at Matsumoto Castle?
No. The official site says drones are prohibited (matsumoto-castle.jp).
Is the Gujo Hachiman "castle in the sky" visible in winter?
The official site says early mornings from October to March often show the castle above a sea of clouds from Horikoshi Pass (gujohachiman.com). Don't stop or set up a tripod on Route 256 (gujohachiman.com).
