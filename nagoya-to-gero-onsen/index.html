Nagoya to Gero Onsen in winter

The Hida train, New Year seat rules, the guests-only bus, and a one-night plan. Official pages first. Recheck before you book.

Gero Onsen sits on the Hida River in Gifu Prefecture, on the JR Takayama Line between Nagoya and Takayama. The Gero Onsen Tourism Association calls it one of Japan’s three famous hot springs, alongside Arima in Hyogo and Kusatsu in Gunma. Its water is listed as an alkaline simple spring with a pH of 9.2. The Japan National Tourism Organization puts the train ride from Nagoya at about 90 minutes.

This page covers getting there in winter — fares, the New Year all-reserved period, a discount round-trip ticket, and the guests-only direct bus — plus winter events and a one-night plan. Public bath hours, prices, towels and footbath details are on the Gero Onsen day trip page.

Getting there by train

The Limited Express Hida

Hida is JR Central’s limited express from Nagoya through Gifu and Gero to Takayama, with some trains continuing to Toyama. JR Central’s ticketless page, checked 25 November 2026, lists the Nagoya–Gero reserved-seat limited express charge at 2,390 yen for adults (1,190 yen for children) and the Green Car at 4,660 yen. Those are normal-season amounts: the same page says 200 yen is taken off in the off-peak season, 200 yen is added in the peak season and 400 yen in the busiest season. They cover the limited express charge only. JR Central says a separate base fare ticket is also needed, so check the total for your date on JR Central’s pages or e5489 before you buy. I am not copying a timetable here, because times change.

JR Central’s all-reserved notice says Hida normally carries one or two unreserved cars. In winter, it plans 100 extra Hida trains toward Gero and Takayama between 1 December 2026 and 28 February 2027, mostly over New Year and on January and February weekends (JR Central winter extra-train release, 22 October 2026).

25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027: every seat is reserved

JR Central’s official notice says every Hida train, on its whole route and including extra trains, runs all-reserved from Friday 25 December 2026 to Tuesday 5 January 2027. In that period:

  • Unreserved limited express tickets cannot be used on Hida.
  • Reserved limited express tickets go on sale at 10:00, one month before the travel date.
  • If your train is full, standing limited express tickets are sold in limited numbers, at the unreserved price, for a named train and car. They are sold at station counters (and at reserved-seat ticket machines from the day before), not on e5489 or Ekinet.

If you are travelling over New Year, book the seat as soon as sales open, and book the return at the same time.

The Hida Area Free Ticket

JR Central’s Hida Area Free Ticket (飛騨エリアフリーきっぷ) costs 12,370 yen from the Nagoya city area (12,370 yen from Gifu, 12,780 yen from Ogaki). It gives a round trip in a Hida ordinary reserved seat and unlimited unreserved travel on JR trains between Hida-Kanayama and Hida-Furukawa, which includes Gero and Takayama, for 3 days. It comes with either a Nohi Bus voucher or a food voucher; one food option is a Gero Onsen sweets coupon. There is no child version.

It cannot be used from 28 December to 6 January, nor from 27 April to 6 May or 10 to 19 August. It is sold only if a reserved seat is available on your outbound Hida. It tends to pay off if you visit Gero and Takayama on the same trip. For a Gero-only round trip, compare it with two single tickets.

Rail passes

The official Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass covers JR trains between Nagoya and Toyama via the Takayama Line, including Limited Express Hida, so Gero sits on its route. It is built for a wider Takayama, Shirakawa-go and Kanazawa loop, and over New Year you still need a (free) reserved-seat ticket for Hida. The direct bus is for ryokan guests only

Nohi Bus’s official highway-bus list, checked 25 November 2026, has no Nagoya–Gero route (its Nagoya line runs to Takayama). The bus you may see mentioned is the Gero Onsen direct bus, run by Minami Hida Kanko Bus for the Gero Onsen Ryokan Cooperative. Its official page, checked 25 November 2026, says:

  • Only guests of cooperative member inns can ride. It is fully reserved, with 45 seats and a minimum of 2 passengers.
  • One round trip a day: Nagoya 14:00 → Gero around 16:30, and Gero 10:30 → Nagoya around 13:00. The Nagoya stop is outside JR Nagoya Station’s Taiko-dori exit, on the Shinkansen side, with no bus-stop sign. Gather 15 minutes early.
  • Fares from December 2026 to March 2027: 3,800 yen one way, 5,000 yen return per person (adults and children aged 3 and over).
  • Book from two months before and no later than two days before travel, online or by phone with the cooperative. Cancellation fees start seven days before.

Check that your inn is a cooperative member before you plan around the bus.

Winter in Gero: what is on

  • Fireworks Musical winter shows (December 2026). Gero City lists Saturday shows on 5, 12 and 19 December, 20:00–20:15, and a Christmas Eve special on 24 December, 20:00–20:30, on the Hida riverbank upstream of Gero Ohashi bridge, about 3 minutes’ walk from Gero Station.
  • Hanabi Monogatari (January–March 2027). Gero City lists fireworks every Saturday from 9 January to 27 March 2027 — 12 shows — from 20:30 for about 10 minutes, on the riverbank downstream of Gero Ohashi. Viewing is free. Shows are cancelled in storms or high water.
  • Illuminations. The same city page lists a winter illumination and snow lanterns from early December to late March.
  • Morning market is closed in winter. The Gero Onsen Tourism Association lists the 2026 Ideyu morning market from 7 March to 5 December, 8:00 to noon. Do not plan on it between December and early March.

Kua Garden Rotenburo posted a notice on 21 November 2026 about changed opening hours on Fireworks Musical days. Check its official page if you plan an evening soak on a fireworks night.

Baths and passes in brief

  • Yumeguri Tegata (湯めぐり手形). A day-bath pass for 3 baths at member ryokan, 2,500 yen (junior-high age and up), valid for six months, with a towel and pouch. Sold at member ryokan, convenience stores and souvenir shops. Each inn sets its own bathing hours, so check before you walk over. Children aged 4 to elementary-school sixth grade can bathe on an adult’s pass, and children 0–3 are free.
  • Public baths. Shirasagi-no-Yu (470 yen, closed Wednesdays) and Kua Garden Rotenburo (800 yen, closed Thursdays), each about 12 minutes’ walk from Gero Station. Full hours and rules are on the Gero Onsen day trip page.
  • Free footbaths. The Ryokan Cooperative’s footbath page lists nine, including Sagi-no-Ashiyu and Ta-no-Kami-no-Ashiyu (both open 24 hours), Venus-no-Ashiyu at Shirasagi-no-Yu, and one inside the Gassho Village. They are free to use, but the Gassho Village and Onsen Museum footbaths need those sites’ admission, and one footbath is closed for now. Bring a small towel.

A day trip from Nagoya in winter

Daylight is short, so leave Nagoya on a morning Hida and come back on a late-afternoon or evening one. Pick both trains on JR Central’s timetable for your date.

  1. Morning: Nagoya to Gero on the Hida, about 90 minutes. Reserve a seat, and on 25 December–5 January you must.
  2. Late morning: the onsen town. Cross the river into the hot-spring district and warm up at a free footbath.
  3. Lunch. Hida beef and hoba miso (miso grilled on a magnolia leaf) are the classic local dishes.
  4. Afternoon: Gero Onsen Gassho Village. Ten thatched gassho houses moved from places such as Shirakawa-go. Open 8:30–17:00 (last entry 16:30), 800 yen for adults. From 31 December to 2 January it opens 9:00–16:00. The official access page lists a bus of about 6 minutes from the JR Gero Station area (100 yen) or a walk of about 20 minutes, uphill. The bus information is dated October 2023, so check it locally.
  5. Late afternoon: a soak. Use a public bath or the Yumeguri Tegata at a member ryokan, then take the train back.

On a Saturday from 9 January to 27 March 2027, staying until the 20:30 fireworks makes for a late return. Check the last convenient Hida first, or stay the night.

A one-night plan

Day 1

  • Arrive by Hida, or on the direct bus at about 16:30 if your inn is a cooperative member.
  • Leave bags at the inn. Visit the Gassho Village before its 16:30 last entry if you arrive early.
  • Ryokan dinner and an evening bath. On December or January–March Saturdays, walk to the river for the fireworks.

Day 2

  • A morning bath. Use the Yumeguri Tegata at two more inns if you bought one.
  • Return to Nagoya on the 10:30 direct bus, or continue north to Takayama on the Hida (JNTO lists about an hour). See Takayama in winter and the Takayama 2-day itinerary.

Onsen manners and winter basics

  • Wash before you get into the bath, keep your small towel out of the water, and tie up long hair.
  • Tattoo rules vary by inn and bath. Ask the facility directly. Private baths (kashikiri) at some ryokan are an option.
  • Expect snow or ice on some winter days. Wear shoes with grip, and expect to take them off often at inns and restaurants.
  • Trains and inns are warm inside. Dress in layers you can remove.

FAQ

How long is the train from Nagoya to Gero Onsen?
About 90 minutes on the JR Takayama Line, according to the Japan National Tourism Organization and the Gero Onsen Tourism Association. Individual Limited Express Hida trains vary, so check the time of your train on JR Central’s official pages.
Do I need a reserved seat on the Hida over New Year?
Yes. JR Central says every Hida train, including extra trains, runs all-reserved from 25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027. Unreserved limited express tickets cannot be used on Hida in that period. If your train is full, standing limited express tickets are sold in limited numbers at station counters (not on e5489 or Ekinet). Reserved seats go on sale at 10:00 one month before the travel date.
What does the Limited Express Hida cost from Nagoya to Gero?
JR Central’s ticketless page lists 2,390 yen (adult, normal season) for an ordinary reserved seat and 4,660 yen for the Green Car between Nagoya and Gero. Seasonal adjustments of minus 200 to plus 400 yen apply. That is the limited express charge. You also need a base fare ticket for the same trip. Check the total for your date on JR Central’s pages or e5489 before you buy.
Is there a direct bus from Nagoya to Gero Onsen?
Only for some overnight guests. The Gero Onsen Ryokan Cooperative’s direct bus is fully reserved and only for guests of member inns. It runs once a day each way: Nagoya 14:00 to Gero around 16:30, and Gero 10:30 to Nagoya around 13:00. From December 2026 to March 2027 the official fare is 3,800 yen one way or 5,000 yen return.
Are there fireworks in Gero in winter?
Yes. Gero City lists the Fireworks Musical winter shows on 5, 12 and 19 December 2026 (20:00–20:15) and a Christmas Eve special on 24 December (20:00–20:30). The 2027 Hanabi Monogatari runs every Saturday from 9 January to 27 March 2027, 20:30–20:40. Shows are cancelled in storms or high water. Viewing is free.
