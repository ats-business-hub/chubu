Kanazawa 1-day itinerary

Garden, castle park, and Higashi Chaya. Official tickets first. Not a Shirakawa-go sprint.

A simple one-day order

  1. Morning at Kenrokuen, with an optional free early entry if you can reach the Renchi-mon or Zuishinzaka gate in time. Then walk the adjoining Kanazawa Castle Park. Official guidance says the castle-park grounds are free; the restored turret and warehouse buildings have their own tickets.
  2. Midday around the garden and castle area. A “Kenrokuen +1” ticket, listed at 500 yen on the official English page, covers the garden plus one more cultural facility in the city — confirm what it includes at the window.
  3. Afternoon in Higashi Chaya District, Kanazawa’s largest historic geisha district, as described by the official Visit Kanazawa guide. Individual teahouses and shops set their own hours; check those pages before you go in.

This is a walking day in a compact centre. I am not copying café or shop hours that I have not confirmed on official venue pages.

The garden is described as open every day of the year. The Shigure-tei teahouse inside the garden is listed 9:00–16:30 (last entry 16:00) and closed 29 December through 3 January. Official early entry is free: from 6:00 in November–February, and you must leave 15 minutes before paid opening. Only two gates are open at that hour. Smoking is prohibited; pets are not allowed.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Kenrokuen, and when is it open in winter?
The official Kenrokuen guidance page lists individual admission at 320 yen for adults (18+) and 100 yen for children aged 6–17. From 16 October through the end of February the garden is open 8:00–17:00. People aged 65 and over enter free with proof of age. Recheck before you go.
Is there a free early-morning entry?
Yes. Official early admission to Kenrokuen and Kanazawa Castle Park is free. From 1 November through the end of February it starts at 6:00. Only the Renchi-mon and Zuishinzaka entrances are open then, and you must leave 15 minutes before paid hours begin.
Should I add Shirakawa-go as a same-day return from Kanazawa?
I do not recommend it. The highway bus exists, but a short stop between two winter journeys is a poor fit with how the village asks visitors to behave. Stay overnight, or go via Takayama.
