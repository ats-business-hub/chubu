Yunoyama Onsen and Gozaisho Ropeway: a winter day trip from Nagoya

Yunoyama Onsen is a hot-spring town at the foot of Mt. Gozaisho in Komono, Mie Prefecture. The Gozaisho Ropeway runs from the town up to the summit park. In winter that makes it one of the simplest ways to get from Nagoya into snow and rime ice and back the same day, with a soak in a hot spring at the end.

This guide covers one trip: Yunoyama Onsen and Mt. Gozaisho in winter. It sets out the official ropeway fares and winter hours, the Kintetsu train and bus route and the direct highway bus as the official pages describe them, and what the summit and the onsen town offer in the cold months. Every price, time and limit links to its official source. If an official page doesn't print a detail, this guide says so. Where the ropeway's English and Japanese pages differ, this guide follows the Japanese page. Official pages were checked on 27 November 2026; this is a researched guide, not a hands-on visit report.

The onsen itself has a long history. Japan's national tourism site says the waters have drawn visitors for over a thousand years. It also says the town is nicknamed the "deer onsen" from a story that the water healed local deer, and that it has about a dozen inns (japan.travel).

Getting there from Nagoya

There are three ways to get there: Kintetsu train plus local bus, the direct highway bus, or car. The official pages describe each one differently, so this section sticks to what they print.

By Kintetsu train and Mie Kotsu bus

Japan's official tourism site gives this route (japan.travel):

  1. From Kintetsu Nagoya, take the Kintetsu Limited Express to Kintetsu Yokkaichi.
  2. Transfer to a local train to Kintetsu Yunoyama Onsen Station.
  3. Take the Mie Kotsu bus to Sanko Yunoyama Onsen.
  4. Walk about 10 minutes to the ropeway.

The same page puts the trip at about 1 hour 20 minutes (japan.travel). Mie's official tourism site describes the bus leg as about 10 minutes from Kintetsu Yunoyama Onsen Station to the "Yunoyama Onsen / Gozaisho Ropeway-mae" stop (kankomie.or.jp). The two pages name different stops, so confirm the stop for your bus on Mie Kotsu's site (sanco.co.jp).

Kintetsu fare: this guide does not quote it. Kintetsu prints fares in a PDF table and a fare-search form rather than as page text, so look up your fare on the official fare search before you travel (ticket.kintetsu.co.jp). Kintetsu's English site sells a KINTETSU RAIL PASS; check its coverage on that site before relying on it for the Yunoyama line (kintetsu.co.jp). To compare passes, see Japan Rail Pass vs Central Japan regional passes.

By direct highway bus

Mie Kotsu runs a Yunoyama Onsen–Nagoya highway bus (sanco.co.jp). Mie's official tourism site describes a direct bus of about 60 minutes from Meitetsu Bus Center to the "Yunoyama Onsen / Gozaisho Ropeway-mae" stop (kankomie.or.jp). Mie Kotsu's route page links to the timetable and fare table rather than printing them, so check those links for times, fares, operating days and any reservation rules (sanco.co.jp).

Passengers who board this bus in Nagoya get a discount coupon for use in Yunoyama Onsen, while supplies last. It gives ¥150 off an adult ropeway round trip and ¥100 off for children, 10% off at the ropeway base-station shop, and ¥100 off day-use onsen admission at listed facilities (sanco.co.jp). The route contact is Mie Kotsu's Yokkaichi office, 059-323-0808 (sanco.co.jp).

By car

The ropeway says it's about a 10-minute drive from the Komono Interchange on the Shin-Meishin Expressway (gozaisho.co.jp). Mie's tourism site puts it at about 60 minutes from Nagoya Nishi IC (kankomie.or.jp). Parking costs ¥1,000 per day for a standard car (gozaisho.co.jp), and the Mie page lists 300 paid spaces (kankomie.or.jp). The ski page does not state winter tire or chain rules, so check road conditions and your rental car's equipment before you drive (gozaisho.co.jp).

Winter operating hours (December 1 to March 31)

The ropeway runs a shorter winter schedule from December 1 to March 31 (gozaisho.co.jp). The English and Japanese pages agree on the opening time and the last ascent but differ on later times. The table shows both; plan on the Japanese page's times, and leave a margin either way.

Ropeway last ascent 16:00 16:00

Sightseeing lift opens 9:30 9:30
Lift last ascent / last descent 15:40 / 16:00 15:50 / 16:10

Sources: gozaisho.co.jp ; gozaisho.co.jp

From April 1 to November 30, the last ascent is 17:00 (gozaisho.co.jp). If you're traveling in late November, the longer hours still apply.

Restaurants and shops at the summit also close earlier in winter. For example, restaurant meal service ends at 15:30 from December to March (gozaisho.co.jp). Mie's tourism site lists irregular closing days (kankomie.or.jp). The ropeway's price page links to a separate suspension notice page for closures (gozaisho.co.jp). Check it, and the live status on the home page, on the morning you travel (gozaisho.co.jp).

Winter at the summit: rime ice, snow play and the ski area

Rime ice. The official ropeway site names rime ice (juhyo) as a winter attraction, along with skiing and sledding (gozaisho.co.jp). Mie's official tourism sites describe rime ice as a winter highlight and mention frost-covered trees and icefalls (kankomie.or.jp; visitmie-japan.travel). Rime ice depends on cold, moist, windy conditions, so it isn't guaranteed on any given day. The ropeway home page shows summit temperature and wind (gozaisho.co.jp).

The ski area and snow play. Mie's tourism site calls the Gozaisho Ski Area the only ski area in Mie Prefecture (kankomie.or.jp). Japan's national tourism site calls it the closest ski resort to Nagoya (japan.travel). The official ski page shows the 2025–26 season, which ended on March 15, 2026. When checked on 27 November 2026, the page had not yet posted 2026–27 dates, lift prices or rental prices (gozaisho.co.jp).

According to the ski page, the summit area has a beginner slope, two gentle sled slopes, and a 1,000 m snowshoe course of about 90 minutes on the west side of the summit park. Check the ski page to see whether these run in 2026–27 (gozaisho.co.jp). Rentals are handled at the Gozaisho Nature School rental shop, and snow boot sizes are limited (gozaisho.co.jp).

Other summit sights. Japan's national tourism site lists paved walkways, the Gozaisho Nature School, three observatories, a gift shop, and a restaurant serving Gozaisho curry udon. From the gondola you can also see a square rock balanced on two pillars, which locals say resembles Jizo (japan.travel). On a clear day, the onsen association says the views reach over the Ise Plain, Ise Bay and the Chita Peninsula (yunoyama-onsen.com).

Winter events. The ropeway home page lists a Gozaisho Peak Elevation Anniversary on December 12 and a "Christmas Nighttime Operation 2026"; check its event page for the dates (gozaisho.co.jp). Mie's tourism site also notes night operation in winter (kankomie.or.jp).

Exploring Yunoyama Onsen town

The onsen association's official site lists several spots worth fitting in before or after the ropeway (yunoyama-onsen.com):

  • Nakanoshima Park, a forest park surrounded by mountain streams, suited to a walk from the hot-spring town.
  • Oishi Park, known for a granite boulder said to be Japan's largest and for the rocks of the Mitaki River gorge. It's a strong subject in flat winter light.
  • Namidabashi ("Tear Bridge"), named for bathhouse attendants who said tearful goodbyes to departing guests.
  • Sangakuji Temple, once a mountain Buddhist center, now known for prayers for good relationships tied to a folded-crane legend.
  • Kirakudo, beside the ropeway base station, which sells Oishi-yaki, a hand-baked snack made with hot-spring water, for ¥150 per piece (yunoyama-onsen.com).
  • Komono Pottery Village, with a working 12-chamber climbing kiln and pottery workshops for beginners.

Day-use bathing. Neither the onsen association's home page nor its activities page lists day-use bath facilities, hours or fees, so check with each inn before you go (yunoyama-onsen.com; yunoyama-onsen.com). The highway bus coupon mentioned above includes ¥100 off day-use admission at listed facilities (sanco.co.jp). In early 2026 the association ran a winter stamp rally and a "Wakuwaku Onsen Ticket" from January 5 to March 25. Check the association's site for anything similar in 2027 (yunoyama-onsen.com).

A sample winter day

This outline uses only the official anchor times. Fill in train and bus times from Kintetsu (kintetsu.co.jp) and Mie Kotsu (sanco.co.jp) before you go.

  1. Morning: leave Nagoya. Take the Kintetsu route via Yokkaichi, or the direct highway bus from Meitetsu Bus Center (japan.travel; kankomie.or.jp).
  2. Late morning: ride up. The ropeway's first ascent is 9:00. Going up earlier gives you more time at the summit and a better chance of rime ice before the day warms (gozaisho.co.jp).
  3. Midday: summit park. Walk the paved paths and observatories. Have lunch before meal service ends at 15:30 (gozaisho.co.jp).
  4. Afternoon: ride down well before the cutoff. The last winter ascent is 16:00, and the Japanese page gives the last descent as 16:30 (gozaisho.co.jp).
  5. Late afternoon: onsen town. Walk to Oishi Park or Nakanoshima Park, pick up Oishi-yaki at Kirakudo, then go for a day-use soak (yunoyama-onsen.com).
  6. Evening: back to Nagoya.

If you're carrying bags, store them in Nagoya before you leave or use the ropeway station's lockers. Near Nagoya Station, you can compare luggage storage on Radical Storage (affiliate link, #ad).

Practical notes: luggage, restrooms, pets and weather

  • Lockers and baggage storage. The Japanese price page lists coin lockers at ¥400–¥800 in the base and summit station buildings, and staffed storage for large bags at ¥1,000 (gozaisho.co.jp). The English page shows lower, older-looking figures, so go by the Japanese page.
  • Pets. Pets must ride in a carrier. The Japanese page charges ¥1,000 round trip or ¥500 one way per carrier, in effect since October 1, 2020 (gozaisho.co.jp).
  • Restrooms. Restrooms are open only during ropeway operating hours, and the one at the summit station is closed in winter (kankomie.or.jp).
  • Gondolas. Each gondola holds 8–10 people (kankomie.or.jp).
  • Weather and suspensions. Check the live status on the ropeway home page and the suspension notice page linked from the price page before you set off (gozaisho.co.jp; gozaisho.co.jp). The ropeway phone number is 059-392-2261 (kankomie.or.jp).
  • Town notices. Komono Town's official site has municipal information (www2.town.komono.mie.jp).

Frequently asked questions

How much is the Gozaisho Ropeway in winter?
The fare is the same all year: ¥2,600 round trip for adults (junior high and up) and ¥1,300 for children aged 4 to elementary school. Children under 4 ride free (gozaisho.co.jp). Special charges may apply during events (gozaisho.co.jp).
What time does the Gozaisho Ropeway close in winter?
From December 1 to March 31, the last ascent is 16:00. The Japanese price page gives the last descent as 16:30 (the English page says 16:20), so aim to be on a car down well before then (gozaisho.co.jp).
How long does it take to get from Nagoya to the Gozaisho Ropeway?
Japan's official tourism site gives about 1 hour 20 minutes by Kintetsu train and Mie Kotsu bus (japan.travel). Mie's tourism site gives about 60 minutes on the direct highway bus from Meitetsu Bus Center (kankomie.or.jp).
How much is the Kintetsu fare from Nagoya to Yunoyama Onsen?
This guide does not quote it. Use Kintetsu's official fare search (ticket.kintetsu.co.jp).
Is the Gozaisho ski area open for 2026–27?
The official ski page had not posted 2026–27 dates or prices when checked on 27 November 2026. It still shows the 2025–26 season, which ended March 15, 2026 (gozaisho.co.jp).
