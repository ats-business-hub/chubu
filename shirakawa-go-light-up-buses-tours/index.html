The 41st Shirakawa-go winter light-up runs on four evenings only: Monday 11 January 2027 (a public holiday), and Sundays 17, 24 and 31 January 2027, with the lights on from 17:30 to 19:30. Shirakawa Village says the event is fully reserved and ticketed. Without a car, the realistic way in is a dedicated light-up bus sold by a regional bus company. This page compares those buses. Event rules, parking sales and the lodging lottery are in Shirakawa-go winter light-up 2027. Checked 26 November 2026; recheck the operator page before you pay.

Why a light-up bus, and not a regular bus

Shirakawa-go is a lived-in village. Its official English visitor page says night sightseeing is not available to day visitors, and only guests staying in village accommodation see it after dark. The light-up is the exception, and it is controlled: Shirakawa Village says the 2027 event uses advance reservation and tickets only, and that it is limited to four sessions because of snow shortages linked to warming and the time of sunset.

Getting home is the other problem. Nohi Bus says on both its English and Japanese light-up pages that there are no fixed-route buses back to Takayama City after the light-up, and that its light-up bus is the way to see the event by bus. Nohi also announced that fares and timetables on its regular Takayama–Shirakawa-go route are revised from 1 December 2026, so plan day-time trips from the revised timetable.

From Takayama: Nohi Bus Courses A, B and C

Nohi runs three courses from Takayama Nohi Bus Center, next to JR Takayama Station. Adults and children aged 6–12 pay the same fare.

  • Course A (13,000 yen): round-trip bus plus admission to the Gassho-zukuri Minkaen open-air museum with zenzai (sweet red-bean soup). You see the lights from inside the village. No observation deck.
  • Course B (16,000 yen): round-trip bus, an Ogimachi Castle ruins observation deck ticket and a Shirakawa-go souvenir. This is Nohi’s only course with deck access. Nohi says seats are limited and the walk up takes about 20 minutes, uphill.
  • Course C (14,000 yen): round-trip bus, a Hirase Onsen Shiramizu-no-yu bath ticket with towel, and a souvenir. The bath ticket is for use from the day after the tour. Nohi says that to bathe on the tour day you would take the 17:04 local bus from Hirase Onsen into the village instead. No observation deck.

Nohi’s boarding rules: exchange your ticket at the Takayama Nohi Bus Center counter before boarding (it opens at 7:00 on the day and gets busy); no boarding or getting off at intermediate stops and no one-way use; children under 6 ride free without a seat but may be refused when the bus is full, so Nohi recommends buying them a seat. Staying in Takayama? See Takayama in winter and Takayama Station luggage storage.

From Kanazawa: Hokutetsu

Hokuriku Railroad runs one light-up bus from Kanazawa Station West Exit at 14:30, via the Tokai-Hokuriku Expressway, reaching Shirakawa-go Bus Terminal at 16:30 for about 3 hours 20 minutes (to 19:50), and returning to Kanazawa Station at 21:50. The fare is 18,500 yen for adults and 16,500 yen for children, including the round-trip bus and an observation deck ticket; meals are not included.

It is the only operator here that also takes phone bookings, through the Hokutetsu Group Travel Center (0120-316-100 or 076-287-5180; 10:00–12:00 and 13:00–15:00, closed Saturdays, Sundays and holidays). The limit is 4 people per application, and web bookings are paid by credit card only. More on the daytime route in Kanazawa to Shirakawa-go.

From Takaoka: Kaetsuno Bus and Iruka Kotsu

Kaetsuno Bus offers an S Course (observation deck, 40 seats, 18,000 / 16,000 yen) and an A Course (village only, 12,000 / 10,000 yen, three buses to choose from). Meals are not included. Booking is online only on Hassha Oraine Net, up to 6 people per booking, with payment straight away; unpaid bookings are cancelled automatically.

Iruka Kotsu sells one course with an Ogimachi Castle ruins observation deck ticket and a souvenir: 18,000 yen for adults (12 and over, junior high and up) and 16,000 yen for elementary school children. Booking is web only (Hassha Oraine Net), up to 6 people, with immediate payment. Iruka says the walk to the deck is about 20 minutes each way.

How booking works

  • Sales opened in four stages at 14:00 Japan time: 13 October 2026 for 11 January, 19 October for 17 January, 26 October for 24 January and 2 November for 31 January. All four dates are now on sale, so the question is availability.
  • Where: Nohi links its three courses to Japan Bus Online from the English page. Kaetsuno, Iruka and Chitetsu use Hassha Oraine Net. Hokutetsu sells online and by phone.
  • Payment: Nohi requires a credit card at booking. Kaetsuno and Iruka require immediate payment.
  • Group size: up to 6 per booking for Nohi, Kaetsuno and Iruka; up to 4 for Hokutetsu.
  • Sold out? Nohi says it has no waiting list, but cancelled seats return to the booking system automatically, so check the page from time to time.

Cancellations and missed buses

  • Nohi: no changes after payment. Cancelling before departure costs a 100-yen refund processing fee; no cancellations after departure.
  • Kaetsuno: 500 yen per person up to 11 days before; 20% from 10 to 8 days before; 30% from 7 days before to the day before; 50% on the day up to 2 hours before departure; 100% within 2 hours. Buses leave on time, and arriving late (including because a connecting train or bus was late) is generally not refunded, except for cancellations due to natural disasters.
  • Iruka: buses leave on time, and there is no refund if you miss the departure.
  • Hokutetsu and Chitetsu: the cancellation terms are not in the text of their light-up pages. Read the terms shown when you book.

The observation deck, the walk and the cold

  • Tickets only come with a tour. Nohi and Kaetsuno say observation deck tickets are not sold or handed out on site. If you want the classic view from above, choose Nohi B, Hokutetsu, Kaetsuno S or Iruka.
  • It is a walk. Nohi says about 20 minutes uphill; Iruka says about 20 minutes each way. The Shirakawa-go Tourist Association runs a reservation-only shuttle for people who have difficulty walking; book it with the association (05769-6-1013), not the bus company.
  • Ice and cold. Operators warn of very slippery paths and severe cold after dark, and ask for warm clothing and snow-ready, non-slip shoes. See What to wear in Shirakawa-go and Takayama in winter.
  • Snow delays. Heavy snow or traffic can delay the bus and shorten your time in the village.

Which bus fits

  • Based in Takayama, want the view from above: Nohi Course B (16,000 yen, back 20:45).
  • Based in Takayama, happy in the village: Nohi Course A (13,000 yen) is the lowest Takayama fare and skips the uphill walk.
  • Based in Takayama, want an onsen ticket: Nohi Course C, remembering the bath ticket is for the next day onward.
  • Based in Kanazawa: Hokutetsu (18,500 yen, deck included, back 21:50).
  • Based in Takaoka: compare Kaetsuno S, Kaetsuno A and Iruka.
  • Departing the Toyama area: read Chitetsu’s flyer for stops and fares.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a regular bus back to Takayama after the light-up?
No. Nohi Bus says there are no fixed-route buses back to Takayama City after the light-up, and that its light-up bus is the way to see the event by bus.
Can I buy an observation deck ticket in Shirakawa-go on the night?
No. Nohi Bus and Kaetsuno Bus say deck tickets are not sold or handed out on site. Choose a course that includes the deck: Nohi Course B, Hokutetsu, Kaetsuno S Course or Iruka Kotsu.
My date is sold out. Is there a waiting list?
Nohi Bus says it has no waiting list, but cancelled seats return to the booking system automatically, so check the booking page from time to time.
Which light-up bus can I book by phone?
Hokutetsu, from Kanazawa, takes phone bookings through the Hokutetsu Group Travel Center (0120-316-100 or 076-287-5180, weekdays 10:00–12:00 and 13:00–15:00). Nohi, Kaetsuno and Iruka are web only.
