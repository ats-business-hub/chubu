Airalo

Airalo’s Japan page names SoftBank as the primary network, “+2 others.” The unlimited packages it listed were 3 days $11.50, 5 days $17.00, 7 days $26.50, 10 days $34.50, 15 days $48.00, and 30 days $69.00. Fixed-data packages sit under a separate filter that I did not record. Its FAQ says that in many cases you can top up an existing eSIM instead of buying a new one. Read the plan’s fair-use and hotspot notes before you buy.

Saily

Saily’s Japan page listed 1 GB for 7 days at $3.99, then 30-day plans of 3 GB ($7.99), 5 GB ($10.99), 10 GB ($17.99), and 20 GB ($24.99). Its Unlimited plan can be set from 1 to 30 days (15 days showed $48.99) and gives 5 GB a day at full 4G/5G speed, then 1 Mbps for the rest of the day. Saily names KDDI and SoftBank as partners, allows hotspot sharing, and says plans activate automatically when you arrive (with the eSIM line on and roaming enabled), with a 180-day activation period. Saily itself notes that coverage in remote areas such as the Japanese Alps is patchier — relevant for Takayama, Kamikochi, and Shirakawa-go valleys.

Japan Wireless

Japan Wireless is a Japan-based seller. Its eSIM page says plans start “from $2/day,” with 1-day to 30-day options, and its structured data lists 79 plans priced from $2.00 to $79.99. It says it connects directly with au KDDI and NTT Docomo, offers English support from Japan seven days a week by chat, email, and phone, and has a “Plan B”: if your compatible eSIM cannot connect after guided checks, it ships a free pocket Wi-Fi to your accommodation. I have not tested that guarantee.

Also worth a look: Sakura Mobile

Sakura Mobile is another Japan-based seller. Its own comparison says its eSIMs connect directly to Japanese networks (docomo / au) rather than roaming through a foreign carrier. On 25 November 2026 its home page listed the unlimited travel eSIM from 1,375 yen, tax included; the price depends on your dates. Its price page says 3 GB-a-day plans slow to 200 kbps after 3 GB in a day. It also sells a Travel Voice + Data eSIM with a Japanese phone number, which needs in-person ID verification at select airport counters or its Shinjuku office.

Also worth a look: NINJA TRAVEL eSIM

NINJA WiFi, a Japan-based seller (Vision Inc.), also lists a travel eSIM on its official site. The eSIM plan page checked on 27 November 2026 describes NINJA TRAVEL eSIM as a single eSIM that can be topped up and reused, including in 150+ countries, with the same QR code. It says you can apply before or after arriving in Japan, that APN settings are not required, and that a QR code is sent within 24 hours after the order is confirmed. Your phone must be unlocked and eSIM-compatible. Clearly labelled prices on that page were 726 yen for 1 GB and 2,200 yen for 5 GB, tax included; other plan sizes appear on the same page without a complete label in the public listing I recorded, so I am not inventing them. Which one for a Chubu loop?

  • Short city stay in Nagoya: a small fixed-data plan (for example Saily 3 GB for 30 days at $7.99 on 25 November 2026) is usually enough for maps and train searches.
  • Four or five days around Takayama, Shirakawa-go, and Kanazawa: an unlimited or high-allowance plan avoids rationing data on bus days. Compare equal lengths — for example Airalo’s 5-day unlimited at $17.00 against Saily Unlimited set to 5 days and a 5-day Japan Wireless plan.
  • Mountain valleys: no seller guarantees signal everywhere. Download offline maps and save the official bus timetables before you leave Takayama.
  • Want local support or a backup device: Japan Wireless’s Japan-based support and free pocket Wi-Fi Plan B are the distinguishing features.
  • Want a Japan-based eSIM that can later be topped up for other countries: NINJA TRAVEL eSIM is listed as reusable with the same QR code in 150+ countries. Compare the Japan plan length and data amount with the sellers above before you pay.
  • Phone must be unlocked and eSIM-compatible. If it is not, see the pocket Wi-Fi comparison.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best eSIM for a Central Japan trip?
It depends on trip length and data use. As of 25 November 2026, Airalo listed unlimited Japan packages from $11.50 for 3 days on SoftBank (+2 others), Saily listed fixed data from $3.99 and an Unlimited plan with 5 GB a day at full speed on KDDI and SoftBank, and Japan Wireless listed plans from $2 a day on au KDDI and NTT Docomo with Japan-based support.
Will an eSIM work in Takayama, Shirakawa-go, and Kamikochi?
The towns are covered by Japan’s major networks, but no seller guarantees signal in every mountain valley. Saily itself says coverage in remote areas such as the Japanese Alps is patchier. Download offline maps and save official bus timetables.
Can I use my eSIM as a hotspot in Japan?
Saily says hotspot sharing is allowed. For Airalo and Japan Wireless, check the specific plan’s details before you buy.
