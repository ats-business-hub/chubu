Gero Onsen sits on the Takayama line between Nagoya and Takayama, so it works as a day stop or as a soak on the way to a Takayama night. Use official train times and official bath hours. I am describing public day baths from their own operator pages, not hotel day-visit desks, which set their own rules.

Getting there

Ride the Limited Express Hida and get off at Gero Station, or recheck whether a local Takayama-line train fits your day. Official Japan National Tourism Organization text says Gero is about 90 minutes from Nagoya and about an hour from Takayama on this line. I am not copying a fare table — look it up on official JR Central pages for your date. The official Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass covers JR limited expresses on the Nagoya–Takayama corridor; confirm Gero is inside the official map before you rely on it. The highway bus from Nagoya to Takayama is a different product and is not a substitute for this station stop unless an official timetable says it calls at Gero.

Both official public-bath pages say they are about a 12-minute walk from Gero Station. Allow a little more if you are carrying bags or walking in snow.

The same operator lists a combination ticket for both baths at 1,000 yen for adults and 450 yen for elementary-school children (cheaper than paying 1,270 yen separately). Official FAQs say towels are sold, not rented (towel 250 yen, bath towel 600 yen on the published list), cashless payment is not offered at present, and the rest area cannot be used without paying the bathing fee. Shirasagi-no-Yu does not provide shampoo or soap in the bath (small bottles are sold at reception); Kua Garden says shampoo, rinse and body soap are provided. Both sites note stairs between reception, changing rooms and the baths; Shirasagi-no-Yu has a ground-floor changing room for visitors who cannot use the stairs — ask at the front desk.

A free footbath, Venus no Ashiyu, sits in front of Shirasagi-no-Yu; the official page asks you to bring a towel to dry your feet. Bring a small towel anyway, and follow ordinary onsen manners: wash before you enter the water, and keep towels out of the bath. I am not stating a tattoo policy for these two public baths, because their official pages I checked do not publish one — ask the facility if you need a clear rule. For hotel baths, cover seals and private baths, see Gero Onsen with tattoos.

Winter short hours at Kua Garden Rotenburo. An official notice dated 21 November 2026, rechecked 26 November 2026, says Kua Garden is in a restricted area during the Gero Onsen Fireworks Musical winter performances, so the bath runs a shorter day on Saturday 5 December, Saturday 12 December, and Saturday 19 December 2026: last admission 18:15, closing 19:00. The regular hours in the table above (8:00–20:45, last entry 20:00, closed Thursdays) still apply on other days, unless a later official notice says otherwise. Recheck the official hours-change notice before you plan a late-afternoon soak on those Saturdays. I did not find a matching short-hours notice for Shirasagi-no-Yu on the official site today.

If you are starting from Nagoya, the Hida also stops at Gero on the way to Takayama; see Nagoya to Takayama: Hida train, luggage, or highway bus for the same corridor, and Nagoya to Gero Onsen for the winter transfer notes already on this site.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get to Gero Onsen from Nagoya or Takayama?
Gero Station is on JR Central’s Takayama Main Line. The usual official train is the Limited Express Hida. The official Japan National Tourism Organization page says the ride is about 90 minutes from Nagoya and about an hour from Takayama. Confirm that your service stops at Gero, and the fare, on official JR Central pages before you buy.
What does a public bath cost, and when is it open?
Shirasagi-no-Yu’s official page lists adults 470 yen, 10:00–20:45 (last entry 20:00), closed Wednesdays (or Friday if Wednesday is a holiday). Kua Garden Rotenburo lists adults 800 yen, 8:00–20:45 (last entry 20:00), closed Thursdays — except on 5, 12 and 19 December 2026, when an official notice shortens Kua Garden to last admission 18:15 and closing 19:00 because of the winter fireworks musical. A combination ticket for both baths is listed at 1,000 yen for adults. Both official pages, rechecked 26 November 2026, say there is no towel rental (towels are sold) and no cashless payment at present. Recheck before you go.
Can I use the rest area without bathing?
The official FAQs for both Shirasagi-no-Yu and Kua Garden say no. Even if you do not bathe, the regular admission is charged.
