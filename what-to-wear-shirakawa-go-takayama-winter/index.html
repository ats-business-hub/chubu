Takayama and Shirakawa-go sit in the mountains of Gifu Prefecture. Official tourism pages treat December through March as snow country: streets ice over, buses run late in heavy snow, and footwear matters as much as a warm coat. The notes below come from the village, the official Hida Takayama tourism site, and the village’s SHIRAKAWA-Going guide. I have not walked these streets for this article, and I am not inventing a temperature chart of my own.

For winter opening hours and bus slack in Takayama, see Takayama in winter. For the separate, reservation-only January light-up, see Shirakawa-go winter light-up 2027.

Takayama: official winter clothing

Hida Takayama’s official English climate page (HIDA TAKAYAMA) says the city hall area sits on a plateau about 573 metres above sea level. It compares the climate to northern Honshu and Hokkaido: cool nights even in summer, and a lot of snow and cold in winter, with a wide gap between daytime and night-time temperatures and relatively low humidity.

For December, January, and February, that page says a warm coat or jacket is essential, and that you may also want gloves and a hat. It strongly recommends a pair of non-skid boots so you can walk safely on snow and ice. March is described as warmer but still chilly, with a reasonably warm coat or jacket. November “may start snowing, depending on the year,” and a warm coat or jacket is still described as handy.

That is seasonal clothing advice from the official tourism site, not a forecast for your travel dates. Recheck conditions before you go.

Shirakawa-go: coats, boots, and no heels

Shirakawa Village’s official English winter page says snow falls from around late December until early March, and that visitors should wear warm clothes and long boots or footwear with deep treads, because icy roads can be slippery.

The village FAQ (English) adds more detail. Winter temperatures may fall below freezing even during the day. It asks visitors to wear a warm coat and other appropriate clothing such as gloves. It notes frequent accidents from slipping on snow and ice, even when snow is not actually falling. It asks visitors to refrain from wearing heels and to use appropriate winter footwear such as snow-type boots. It also recommends rainwear or a poncho rather than an umbrella when it is snowing.

A first-visit page from the same official site repeats the winter clothing advice and recommends waterproofed coats, plus long boots or warm shoes with grippy soles. It warns that at night there is a risk of slipping on frozen streets, and that fresh snow can hide holes, ponds, or irrigation ditches — so do not step onto uncleared fresh snow.

How cold can a daytime visit feel?

The village’s official travel guide, SHIRAKAWA-Going, describes Shirakawa-go as snow country from December and says some days can reach −5°C, with snow and wind. It recommends very warm clothing such as down jackets and waterproof boots. It also notes that socks and gloves can get wet on snow-covered roads, and tells visitors to bring extra clothing and to take wet clothes home.

That −5°C figure is the guide’s own wording for some days, not a daily average I calculated. Light-up evenings are a colder, separate case (below).

Shoes, in one place

  • Takayama’s official climate page: non-skid boots, strongly recommended in December–February.
  • Shirakawa Village official winter and first-visit pages: long boots or deep-tread / grippy soles; waterproofed coats recommended.
  • Shirakawa Village official FAQ: no heels; snow-type boots; poncho or rainwear instead of an umbrella in falling snow.
  • SHIRAKAWA-Going: waterproof boots, plus spare socks and gloves because they can get wet.

None of these pages names a shoe brand. I am not recommending a model.

Light-up evenings are colder

The Shirakawa-go Tourism Association’s outline for the 2027 light-up, already cited on this site’s light-up guide, describes event evenings as the coldest part of the night, with temperatures from about −10°C to 0°C and icy ground. It recommends warm, waterproof boots, spikes, and a flashlight, and requires studless winter tyres on cars. Drones are prohibited in this living village.

The light-up is held only on four reserved evenings, not every night. A regular highway-bus ticket is not an event ticket. Details and booking rules are on Shirakawa-go winter light-up 2027.

Driving and buses (clothing is not the only winter constraint)

The village FAQ says studless snow tyres are required in winter, and it also recommends tyre chains and four-wheel-drive. SHIRAKAWA-Going says winter tyres are generally fitted from the beginning of December to the end of March, because melted snow can freeze and make roads slippery even before snow settles. Highway winter-tyre rules are set by the road operator; check NEXCO Central or the official road information before you drive.

If you use the highway bus instead, Nohi’s official English page (cited on the winter Takayama guide) warns that heavy snow or expressway closures can delay buses by two hours or more. Do not cut a last bus close. Official bus and light-up pages come first, before any partner booking.

Frequently asked questions

What shoes should I wear in Shirakawa-go in winter?
Shirakawa Village’s official English pages advise against heels and recommend long boots, snow-type boots, or shoes with a deep, grippy tread, because streets are often icy even when it is not snowing. The village’s SHIRAKAWA-Going guide also recommends waterproof boots. This is official guidance, not a product test.
How cold is Takayama in December, January, and February?
Hida Takayama’s official climate page says Takayama sits at about 573 metres above sea level and is very cold and snowy in winter, with a wide gap between daytime and night-time temperatures. For December, January, and February it says a warm coat or jacket is essential, and it strongly recommends non-skid boots, plus gloves and a hat. This page does not invent a temperature table of its own.
Is light-up evening clothing different from a daytime winter visit?
Yes. The Shirakawa-go Tourism Association’s 2027 light-up outline describes event evenings as the coldest part of the night, about −10°C to 0°C, with icy ground, and recommends warm waterproof boots, spikes, and a flashlight. A daytime visit still needs winter footwear, but the evening event is the colder case. Light-up entry is reservation-only and is not the same as a regular highway-bus ticket.
