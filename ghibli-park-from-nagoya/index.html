Ghibli Park from Nagoya

Reserve official tickets first. Public transport only is recommended; there is no dedicated park parking.

Ghibli Park, in Expo 2005 Aichi Commemorative Park, is not the Ghibli Museum in Mitaka. You generally cannot buy admission at the gate. Official English ticket pages, checked 22 November 2026, say a reservation is always required, and that tickets are not sold at the park except for some same-day tickets to individual buildings in the Valley of Witches area if you do not already hold an O-Sanpo Premium ticket. Start with the official ticket page, then compare a partner cart only if the date and ticket type match.

Official hours

Official ticket and access pages list park hours as 10:00–17:00 on weekdays and 9:00–17:00 on Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays, with final entry to areas at 16:30. Recheck your date; hours can vary with operations.

Official ticket types and prices

Yen figures below are from the official English ticket page, checked 22 November 2026. Prices include tax. A service charge is added at issuance. Ages 0–3 enter free. Recheck the live official page; sales rules are strict.

Standard (Grand Warehouse, Mononoke Village, Valley of Witches)3,300 / 1,650 yen3,800 / 1,900 yen

If you do not hold a Premium ticket, official same-day building tickets — only when sold — are listed as Okino 400 / 200 yen, Howl’s Castle 1,000 / 500 yen, and House of Witches 400 / 200 yen (adult / child). These do not replace a reserved park ticket.

Official sales open two months ahead, on the 10th of the month at 14:00 Japan time. The official page, on this check, said February 2027 tickets go on sale at 14:00 on 10 December 2026. The limit is six tickets per purchase and one purchase per ticket type per month. Official conditions say no refunds or date changes, identification may be checked, and resale is banned.

How to get there from Nagoya

The official Japanese access page (the English /en/access/ URL returned 404 on 22 November 2026) says to use the Linimo (Aichi Rapid Transit Tobu Kyuryo Line) and get off at Expo 2005 Aichi Commemorative Park station, which is next to the park. It also lists a Meitetsu Bus from Meitetsu Bus Center (Nagoya Station), 4th floor, bay 24, bound for Expo 2005 Aichi Commemorative Park (Ghibli Park).

Official bus counts on that page: weekdays, 9 buses outbound from the bus center (7 of them direct) and 8 return (6 direct); weekends and holidays, 10 outbound, all direct, and 9 return, all direct. Some departures are cashless demonstration buses that do not accept cash — the official page listed weekday 8:20 from the bus center and 16:45 from the park, and holiday 7:55 from the bus center and 16:45 from the park, “for the time being.” There are also official buses from Chubu Centrair Airport and from Seto. Check the official Meitetsu Bus site for the current timetable and fare; I am not inventing a yen figure or a travel time from third-party blogs.

Ghibli Park has no dedicated parking. Official guidance recommends public transport. If you must come by car, use the Expo park’s official parking information, and expect congestion around holidays and summer events. Official text notes that daily reserved attendance is around 5,000 people.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy a ticket at the park?
Generally no. Official ticket pages say a reservation is always required, and tickets are not sold at the park except for some same-day tickets to individual Valley of Witches buildings if you do not already hold an O-Sanpo Premium ticket.
How much are official tickets?
As of 22 November 2026, O-Sanpo Premium is listed at 7,300 yen on weekdays and 7,800 yen on weekends and holidays for adults, and half those amounts for children aged 4–12. Standard tickets are 3,300 / 3,800 yen for adults on the same pattern. A service charge is added. Recheck the official page.
How do I get there from Nagoya Station?
Official access guidance names the Linimo to Expo 2005 Aichi Commemorative Park station, and a Meitetsu Bus from Meitetsu Bus Center 4th floor, bay 24. Check official Meitetsu Bus timetables and fares; some runs are cashless only. There is no dedicated Ghibli Park parking.
Is this the Ghibli Museum?
No. Ghibli Park in Aichi is a separate site from the Ghibli Museum in Mitaka, Tokyo. Tickets are not interchangeable.
