Matsumoto Castle: tickets, hours, and tips

Timed official e-tickets cost less than the paper ticket. The keep is 140 steep steps, with no elevator.

Matsumoto Castle is a national-treasure keep, and a realistic half-day from Matsumoto Station if you book a timed ticket and allow for the stairs. Buy official tickets first. Kamikochi, nearby in season, is closed until 17 April 2027, so do not treat this as a combined castle-and-Kamikochi winter day.

Regular hours are 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., last entry 4:30 p.m. The official page says visitors spend about 45 to 60 minutes inside the keep. The castle is closed 29–31 December. Golden Week extended hours are listed as 8:00 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., last entry 5:30 p.m. The Japanese official page also gives 10:00–15:30 (last entry 15:00) for the first three days of January — confirm that if your visit falls there. Visitors with a disability certificate, plus one caregiver, enter free at the window. Official pages say there is no re-entry once you leave the grounds.

Combo e-tickets with other city museums exist and are sold only online, not at the castle ticket office. They have their own validity dates. I am not listing every combo here; use the official purchase page and check each facility’s closing days first. E-tickets cannot be cancelled after purchase.

Practical tips from the official pages

  • Enter through the Kuromon Gate on the south side. The red Uzumibashi Bridge at the rear is closed as an entrance.
  • The keep has no elevator. Official text says you climb about 140 steps at a maximum grade of 61° to reach the sixth floor. If steep historic stairs are not for you, enjoy the grounds and skip the keep rather than starting the climb.
  • Shoes come off inside. Bags for shoes are handed out at the entrance; you carry them with you.
  • Pets, including those in carriers, are not allowed in the Honmaru Garden or the keep. Certified service dogs need advance permission from the castle office.
  • Do not bring outside food into the grounds. The official page points to the gift shop for drinks and ice cream.
  • On busy Golden Week and Obon days, official pages say entry may be limited for safety, and part of the garden may be used as a queue.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Matsumoto Castle, and should I buy an e-ticket?
The official English information page lists timed-entry e-tickets at 1,200 yen for adults and 400 yen for children aged 6–15, and on-site paper tickets at 1,300 yen for adults (children still 400 yen). Children aged 5 and under enter free. E-tickets are timed entry, sold online, and the official pages say they cannot be cancelled or changed after purchase. Recheck before you pay.
What are the hours, and when is it closed?
Official regular hours are 8:30 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., last entry 4:30 p.m. The castle is open year-round except 29–31 December. The Japanese official page also lists shorter hours for the first three days of January (10:00–15:30, last entry 15:00) — recheck if you are going over the New Year.
Is the keep accessible, and can I re-enter?
The official page says the Great Keep is six storeys, with steep, narrow stairs and no elevator: about 140 steps, with a maximum grade of 61°. Shoes come off inside; you carry them in a bag provided at the entrance. The only entrance to the grounds is the Kuromon Gate; the red Uzumibashi Bridge is closed. If you leave the grounds, official rules say you will not be readmitted without paying again.
