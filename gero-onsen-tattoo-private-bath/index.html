Gero Onsen with tattoos: what official pages say about baths, cover seals, and private baths

Read from the baths’ and the tourism association’s own pages. No assumptions, and not a hands-on test.

Many Japanese onsen still refuse guests with tattoos, and Gero is no exception. Rules are set by each facility and can change. This page sets out what official pages said on 26 November 2026, and where they say nothing. A private bath is not automatically tattoo-friendly: unless a facility says so in writing, ask it directly before you go.

Yu-meguri Tegata (bath-hopping pass)

The Gero Onsen Ryokan Cooperative’s Yu-meguri Tegata is a pass for day bathing at member inns. Its official pages list it at 2,500 yen, valid for 6 months from purchase. Its bathing etiquette page says that guests with tattoos are firmly refused (入れ墨をされた方のご入浴は固くお断り). The same page warns that some inns don’t include certain baths, such as family baths and private open-air baths. So if you have tattoos, the pass isn’t a route into private baths either.

Ooedo Onsen Monogatari: cover seals

The day-trip pages for Ooedo Onsen Monogatari Premium Gero Honkan and Gero Bekkan say guests with tattoos can’t use the large bath, except when the tattoos are fully covered by the hotel’s designated cover seals (paid). The group’s terms of use say entry is refused for tattooed guests unless the tattoos can be fully covered by two of the designated seals. If your tattoos are larger than that, plan on another option.

Fugaku: no tattoos, seals not accepted

Fugaku’s bath page says guests with tattoos can’t use the large bath or the open-air bath, regardless of location, size, or type, and that seals are not accepted either. Day bathing covers the large bath and observation open-air bath only, with reception 18:00–20:00 (adult 800 yen), and may be refused when busy. The page also lists private baths (1,100 yen for 45 minutes, 15:00–22:00, reservation required, once per group), but it doesn’t say day visitors can book them or what the tattoo rule is there.

Suihoen: private bath for day visitors

Suihoen’s bath page says outside visitors (non-staying guests) may use the private bath only. For day visitors, the private open-air bath with indoor bath, Mutsumi-no-Yu, is listed at:

  • 3,000 yen for up to 2 people, plus 1,000 yen per extra person
  • 45 minutes, within 12:00–20:00
  • Apply at the Suihoen front desk on the day, 8:00–19:00. Phone reservations are not accepted.

The page does not state a tattoo rule for this bath. Don’t take that as permission. If tattoos are a concern, ask the front desk before you pay.

Public baths in town

Shirasagi-no-Yu and Kua Garden Rotenburo are the town’s public day baths. Their official pages that we checked do not publish a tattoo policy, so ask at reception if you need a clear answer. Hours and prices are in Gero Onsen day trip.

How to ask

  • Ask before paying, and say where the tattoo is and roughly how big. Rules often depend on whether it can be covered.
  • A short line in Japanese helps: 「タトゥーがありますが、貸切風呂は利用できますか？」 (I have a tattoo. Can I use the private bath?)
  • If you are staying overnight, ask the inn about in-room baths or private baths when you book.
  • Accept a no politely. Facility rules apply even if another bath nearby is more relaxed.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use the Yu-meguri Tegata pass with tattoos?
No. The pass’s official bathing etiquette page says guests with tattoos are firmly refused. It also says some inns exclude family baths and private open-air baths.
Are private baths in Gero tattoo-friendly?
Not automatically. Suihoen’s official page says day visitors may use its private bath (3,000 yen for up to 2 people, 45 minutes, within 12:00–20:00, front desk only), but it does not state a tattoo rule. Ask the facility before you pay.
Can I cover tattoos with a seal?
At Ooedo Onsen Monogatari Premium Gero Honkan and Gero Bekkan, the day-trip pages say you may use the large bath only if tattoos are fully covered by the hotel’s paid cover seals; the group’s terms mention two seals. Fugaku’s page says seals are not accepted.
