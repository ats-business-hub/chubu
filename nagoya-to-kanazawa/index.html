Nagoya to Kanazawa: how to get there

One change at Tsuruga on official JR trains, or a reserved highway bus. Recheck fares for 1 December 2026.

Official operator pages remain the primary place to book. I do not recommend day-tripping Shirakawa-go from Kanazawa and back, and I do not treat the nationwide Japan Rail Pass as the default for this loop.

You can go from Nagoya to Kanazawa by train with one change at Tsuruga, or by a reserved highway bus with no change. The train is usually the more predictable ride. The bus is often the lower official tariff, at the cost of road time. I do not treat the nationwide Japan Rail Pass as the default, and I do not recommend turning this journey into a same-day Shirakawa-go return.

By train, via Tsuruga

Take JR’s Limited Express Shirasagi from Nagoya to Tsuruga, then transfer to the Hokuriku Shinkansen for Kanazawa. Look up the day’s times, the connection at Tsuruga, and the total fare on official JR ticket search — I am not copying a timetable or a yen figure from unofficial sites. JR West’s English ticket-search page says reservations are accepted up to one month before departure, from 10:00 JST on the same day of the preceding month.

A 15 December 2023 JR West press release for the 16 March 2024 timetable change said Shirasagi would operate with all reserved seats, including Nagoya–Tsuruga, with former unreserved cars converted to reserved ordinary seats. Treat that as the working rule and recheck official JR pages for your date. At Tsuruga you change stations between the conventional limited express and the Shinkansen; follow official signs and allow enough time if you have luggage.

By reserved highway bus

Meitetsu Bus, Hokutetsu, JR Tokai Bus and West Japan JR Bus jointly run a reserved-seat highway bus between Meitetsu Bus Center (and, toward Kanazawa, JR Nagoya Station) and Kanazawa Station West Exit. Hokutetsu’s official page for the 1 December 2026 revision lists a required time of 3 hours 49 minutes to 4 hours, including a rest of about 20 minutes at Nanjo SA, over 245 km. All seats require a reservation. Book on highwaybus.com, or by phone with the numbers on the official operator pages. Reservations open one month before the travel date.

Adult one-way calendar fares published by Meitetsu and Hokutetsu for travel from 1 December 2026, checked 22 November 2026. Recheck the calendar for your date. For travel through 30 November 2026, use the operator pages’ pre-revision table.

Meitetsu’s timetable notes that you cannot get off at JR Nagoya Station when travelling toward Kanazawa, and that buses from Kanazawa back to Nagoya do not stop at JR Nagoya Station — they end at Meitetsu Bus Center. Toward Kanazawa, official first and last departures from Meitetsu Bus Center on the published table start at 7:30 and finish at 19:30; from Kanazawa West Exit they start at 6:30 and finish at 18:30. Recheck the live table, including intermediate stops, before you book. This is a different service from the Nagoya–Shirakawa-go–Kanazawa bus; Meitetsu points those passengers to a separate page.

Which should you take?

  • Take the train if you want a clock you can plan around and a reserved seat through the mountains, and you are willing to change at Tsuruga.
  • Take the highway bus if the official calendar fare for your date is the better total and you can live with expressway delays.
  • If you already hold the official Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass, use it on the routes the official map actually covers — often the Hida and the Shirakawa-go buses — rather than assuming it is a Nagoya–Kanazawa Shinkansen ticket.

Frequently asked questions

Is there still a direct limited express from Nagoya all the way to Kanazawa?
No. Since the Hokuriku Shinkansen was extended to Tsuruga in March 2024, the usual official rail route is the Limited Express Shirasagi from Nagoya to Tsuruga, then a Hokuriku Shinkansen service from Tsuruga to Kanazawa. Look up the day’s connection on official JR ticket search.
Do I need a seat reservation?
A March 2024 JR West press release said Shirasagi became an all-reserved-seat train, including the Nagoya–Tsuruga section. Reserve before you board, and recheck the rule for your date on official JR pages. Reserve Shinkansen seats as required for the service you choose.
Is the highway bus cheaper?
Often, yes, but it is slower and subject to traffic. Official operator pages list a running time of 3 hours 49 minutes to 4 hours including a rest stop, and it is reserved-seat only. From 1 December 2026 the official adult calendar fare is 3,600–5,800 yen one way, or 5,800 yen if you pay on board. Recheck the price for dates before that revision on the operator sites.
Does the Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass cover the Shirasagi plus Shinkansen route?
Do not assume that it does. The official pass page lists JR trains between Nagoya and Toyama via the Tokaido and Takayama lines, and the Hokuriku Shinkansen between Toyama and Tsuruga, plus certain buses. Check the official map for your exact trains before you buy. The pass is built for the Takayama–Shirakawa-go loop, not as a default Nagoya–Kanazawa Shinkansen ticket. It is not the nationwide Japan Rail Pass.
