Ise Jingu is one of the most rewarding single-destination day trips from Nagoya, and it's easy to do without a car. This guide covers just the shrine visit. For other destinations, see Day trips from Nagoya.

What Ise Jingu is

Ise Jingu (officially just "Jingu") is a group of Shinto shrines in Ise City, Mie Prefecture. It includes 125 shrines, centered on two main sanctuaries, Naiku and Geku.

  • Naiku (Kotaijingu) is dedicated to Amaterasu-Omikami, the ancestral kami of the Imperial family, enshrined there about 2,000 years ago.
  • Geku (Toyo'ukedaijingu) is dedicated to Toyo'uke-no-Omikami, enshrined about 1,500 years ago and associated with food, clothing, shelter and abundant harvests.

The shrine's English site also explains the Shikinen Sengu. Every twenty years, the divine symbol is moved to a newly built palace, and the Ujibashi Bridge is rebuilt as part of this cycle. Next to each main sanctuary you'll see an empty plot. That's where the next palace will stand.

Official worship hours

Ise Jingu has no official English hours page, so these hours come from the Japanese visitor page. Naiku, Geku and the associated shrines are open 5:00–18:00 in January–April and September, 5:00–19:00 in May–August, and 5:00–17:00 in October–December. Hours change over the New Year period, and the shrine posts those changes on its news page.

For a winter trip, 17:00 is the cutoff. Plan to reach Naiku with plenty of daylight left, as it's the larger of the two and the official model courses for it are longer.

Why the visit starts at Geku

The official course page says plainly that it's customary for an Ise pilgrimage to begin at Geku. The English access page agrees: "Please visit Geku at first, and then visit Naiku."

This works well practically too. Geku sits close to the stations you arrive at from Nagoya, and Naiku is a bus ride further on.

Nagoya to Ise by train

The Ise City Tourism Association's English access page gives one Nagoya route: from Nagoya Station by Kintetsu Limited Express or JR Rapid Mie to Iseshi Station, in about 1 hour 20 minutes. The shrine's Japanese access page lists Kintetsu, JR Central and the Kintetsu sightseeing limited express Shimakaze as the rail options from the Nagoya direction.

Two stations matter for the shrines: Iseshi and Ujiyamada. Both are within walking distance of Geku (see below). Check the timetable for which station your train stops at.

On Kintetsu Limited Express, every seat is reserved, so the tourism association's guide says you need a limited express (reserved-seat) ticket as well as the regular ticket.

Coming off the Shinkansen at Nagoya? The tourism association has a useful transfer tip: it recommends riding near cars 11 to 13 for the quickest connection to the Kintetsu line. From there, go through the transfer gate, turn right down the hallway, then turn left at the dead end and take the stairs to the Kintetsu ticket gate.

Geku: getting there and the model courses

From the stations, Geku is close. The tourism association gives about 5 minutes on foot (or 2 minutes by bus) from Iseshi Station, and 10 minutes on foot from Ujiyamada Station.

The shrine publishes two Geku model courses: a 30-minute course and a 60-minute course. Both start at the Hiyokebashi Bridge.

30-minute course: Hiyokebashi Bridge → purification font (temizusha) → the main sanctuary, Toyo'ukedaijingu → the Mishikichi site → Mitsuishi → the associated shrines Taka-no-miya, Tsuchi-no-miya and Kaze-no-miya → the Geku Kaguraden.

60-minute course: The longer course adds stops such as the Kiyomori camphor tree, the Sengukan museum, Shimo-mii-jinja and the north gate (Kitamikado) area. The Sengukan is only on the 60-minute course.

Course times don't include formal prayer services, and some spots may be off-limits depending on circumstances. For a day trip, the 30-minute course leaves more time for Naiku.

Geku to Naiku: bus, taxi and CAN Bus

The shrines are linked by Mie Kotsu buses. The shrine's access page lists Mie Kotsu route buses 51 and 55, the CAN Bus loop, and an express service that stops only at major stops.

According to the Ise City Tourism Association's English access guide (the shrine's own pages don't list bus fares; the guide's "as of" date was unclear, so check fares on the day):

  • The Geku–Naiku bus usually takes 15 to 20 minutes and costs ¥520 one way.
  • For Naiku, get off at Naiku-mae (¥520). For Okage Yokocho, get off at Jingu Kaikan-mae (¥450).
  • The Shinto Liner limited express doesn't stop at Jingu Kaikan-mae or Sarutahiko Shrine-mae. Pick a regular bus if you want those stops.
  • A taxi between the Outer and Inner Shrines costs about ¥2,500 per car.

The CAN Bus currently departs from Isuzugawa Station and doesn't pass through Iseshi Station. It's mainly useful if you plan to continue to Futami or Toba, which is beyond a simple shrine day trip.

Naiku: the model course

Naiku is where most visitors spend the bulk of their time. The shrine publishes a 60-minute course and a 90-minute course for Naiku.

The 60-minute course starts at the Ujibashi Bridge and runs, as published: Ujibashi Bridge → temizusha and the riverside purification spot (Mitarashi) → Takimatsuri-no-kami → the main sanctuary, Kotaijingu → Mishine-no-mikura → Aramatsuri-no-miya → Kazahinomi-no-miya → Naiku Kaguraden → Sanshuden.

Again, some places may be closed to entry depending on circumstances. Leave room on either side of the course for the walk in from Naiku-mae and for Okage Yokocho, the shopping and food street near the bus stops.

Luggage, IC cards and practical details

  • Payment on buses. Cash, transport IC cards and bus tickets bought at the station bus counter are all accepted. Pass holders such as "Michikusa Ticket" users just show the ticket when getting off.
  • Luggage at Iseshi Station. The fee is a flat ¥600 per piece regardless of size, and items must be collected by 17:30 the same day. If you're carrying a bag between Nagoya hotels, check that deadline against your return train.
  • Coin lockers. There are also coin lockers outside the ticket gates at Isuzugawa Station.
  • Rental bikes. The English access page notes that rental bicycles are available through the Ise City Tourist Information Office.

Crowds and holiday traffic

During the year-end and New Year period, Golden Week and other long holidays, surrounding roads are expected to be congested, and a park-and-bus-ride shuttle runs from temporary parking. The shrine's visitor page also notes that congestion information is available on Rakuraku Ise Mode. That's one more reason to take the train from Nagoya on busy dates.

Shaping your day

The official sources support a simple structure, without needing to fix clock times in advance:

  1. Morning: Kintetsu (or JR Rapid Mie) from Nagoya to Iseshi Station, about 1 hour 20 minutes.
  2. Geku: Walk about 5 minutes from Iseshi Station. Follow the 30- or 60-minute model course.
  3. Bus to Naiku: Usually 15–20 minutes; ¥520 to Naiku-mae per the tourism association.
  4. Naiku and lunch: The 60- or 90-minute model course, plus time in Okage Yokocho.
  5. Return: Bus back toward Iseshi or Ujiyamada, then the train to Nagoya. Buses bound for Iseshi and Ujiyamada stations leave every 10 to 15 minutes.

Check your return departure against the Kintetsu timetable PDF before you set out, and remember the 17:00 closing from October to December. Frequently asked questions

What are Ise Jingu's opening hours?
According to the official Japanese page, 5:00–18:00 in January–April and September, 5:00–19:00 in May–August, and 5:00–17:00 in October–December. Hours change over the New Year period.
Should I visit Geku or Naiku first?
Geku. The shrine's course page calls starting at Geku the custom, and the English access page asks visitors to visit Geku first.
How long does it take to get from Nagoya to Ise?
The Ise City Tourism Association gives about 1 hour 20 minutes from Nagoya Station to Iseshi Station by Kintetsu Limited Express or JR Rapid Mie.
How do I get from Geku to Naiku?
Take a Mie Kotsu bus (routes 51/55). The tourism association says it usually takes 15–20 minutes and costs ¥520 one way to Naiku-mae, and that a taxi is about ¥2,500 per car. Check current fares before you go.
Do I need a reservation for the Kintetsu Limited Express?
All seats on Kintetsu Limited Express trains are reserved, so you need a limited express ticket. The tourism association suggests buying in advance; Kintetsu sells tickets at stations and through its official booking pages.
