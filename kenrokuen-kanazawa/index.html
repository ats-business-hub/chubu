Kenrokuen is Kanazawa’s central landscape garden, next to Kanazawa Castle Park. Official tickets are inexpensive and easy to buy at the gate. This page gathers official hours, admission, early entry, and how the garden fits a one-day or two-day stay. Recheck the official guidance page on the day you go.

The garden is described as open every day of the year. Official Japanese guidance also lists last-entry times and free-admission days, including around 31 December–3 January — recheck that Japanese page if you are visiting over New Year, because the English table I fetched does not spell those free days out in the same way. Coupons cannot be used, according to the English page.

The Shigure-tei teahouse inside the garden is listed 9:00–16:30 (last entry 16:00) and is closed 29 December through 3 January. Smoking is prohibited in the park. Pets are not allowed. Wheelchairs are available free of charge at listed gates; the official page notes that the garden has many slopes.

Free early admission

Official early admission to Kenrokuen and Kanazawa Castle Park is free if you enter during the early window and leave 15 minutes before paid hours begin. Only the Renchi-mon and Zuishinzaka entrances are open then. Official English start times:

  • 1–31 March: from 5:00
  • 1 April–31 August: from 4:00
  • 1 September–31 October: from 5:00 (the English page typographically shows “ept. 1”)
  • 1 November–end of February: from 6:00

This is the quiet way to see the garden in any season, especially in winter when paid hours start at 8:00. Plan your exit; staying past the leave-by time is not what the free entry allows.

Kanazawa Castle Park next door

Official combined guidance says the castle-park grounds are free and share the same park hours idea as the garden. Restored turret and warehouse buildings have separate tickets and hours — official English text has listed building hours around 9:00–16:30 with last entry at 16:00, and a 320 / 100 yen ticket, but recheck the official combined page before you count on going inside. The grounds themselves are enough for a walk between the garden and the city.

How to fit Kenrokuen into a Kanazawa day

For a single day, start at the garden (or at early entry), walk the castle park, and continue to Higashi Chaya, as in the one-day itinerary. A second day is a better time for another official cultural facility on the Kenrokuen +1 ticket. I do not recommend adding a same-day Shirakawa-go return onto a garden morning; keep the village as a one-way departure or a later overnight.

Frequently asked questions

How much is Kenrokuen, and when is it open in winter?
The official English guidance page lists individual admission at 320 yen for adults (18+) and 100 yen for children aged 6–17. From 16 October through the end of February the garden is open 8:00–17:00. People aged 65 and over enter free with proof of age. Recheck before you go.
Is there a free early-morning entry?
Yes. Official early admission is free. From 1 November through the end of February it starts at 6:00. Only the Renchi-mon and Zuishinzaka entrances are open then, and you must leave 15 minutes before paid hours begin.
What is the Kenrokuen +1 ticket?
Official English guidance lists it at 500 yen and says it allows admission to Kenrokuen plus one more cultural facility in the city. Confirm what it includes at the window.
Can I pay by card?
The official English guidance page still says cash payments only. The official Japanese guidance lists additional payment methods, including cards and e-money. Confirm at the window on the day.
