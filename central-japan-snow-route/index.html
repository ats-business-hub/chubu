Central Japan snow route: Nagoya, Takayama, Shirakawa-go and Kanazawa

A winter-to-spring planning hub. Official buses, trains, and pass rules first. Recheck before you book.

Official operator pages remain the primary place to book. I do not recommend day-tripping Shirakawa-go from Kanazawa and back, and I do not treat the nationwide Japan Rail Pass as the default for this loop.

The corridor from Nagoya through Takayama and Shirakawa-go to Kanazawa is one of the more straightforward overland routes in Central Japan between December and March. It links a Tokaido Shinkansen and airport hub with Hida merchant streets, a thatched-roof mountain village, and Kanazawa’s gardens and tea-house districts. Tourism materials sometimes call this wider circuit the Shoryudo or the Takayama–Hokuriku route.

Winter and early spring need different logistics than autumn. Highway buses into Shirakawa-go often require advance seat reservations, snow and ice change walking conditions from month to month, and the official Shirakawa-go winter light-up is completely reservation-only. This page is a planning hub — routing, pacing, month-by-month expectations, and transport strategy. City-by-city walking plans and detailed booking steps live in the linked guides.

Why travel this route in winter to early spring?

  • Rail and bus that are built to keep running. Limited Express Hida trains run directly from Nagoya to Takayama. Highway buses, operated with Nohi Bus and partner companies, are the public way into Shirakawa-go and onward toward Kanazawa. From Kanazawa, the Hokuriku Shinkansen continues toward Tokyo, or you transfer at Tsuruga for conventional limited expresses toward Kyoto and Osaka. See the transport section below for official coverage.
  • A short climb from a mild city winter into snow country. Nagoya is often dry and chilly in midwinter, while Takayama and Shirakawa-go can hold deep snow, and Kanazawa sits on the Sea of Japan side. Conditions still vary by week; I am not promising snowfall on any given date.
  • Seasonal food. Winter is the usual season for Hida beef hotpots, sake from Takayama’s brewery streets, and cold-weather seafood in Kanazawa, including snow crab and yellowtail when it is in season. Check opening days — many smaller shops close over the New Year holiday.

Month by month: December to March

Snow, daylight, and demand change across these four months. Choose a month for the kind of winter you want — early snow and quieter streets, the official light-up dates, or milder walking later in the season — not for a guaranteed postcard scene.

Month What to expect Demand and planning notes
December Early snow is common in Takayama and Shirakawa-go by mid-to-late December, but deep drifts are not guaranteed in the first half of the month. Nagoya usually stays chilly and relatively dry. Quieter early in the month. Year-end travel and shop closures cluster around the New Year holiday. On the official pass page, all seats on Limited Express Hida trains are reserved only from 25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027.
January Typically the core of the snow season in Hida and Shirakawa-go. Kanazawa often sees wet snow or cold rain as well as snow. The official 2027 Shirakawa-go light-up is entirely in January (11, 17, 24 and 31 January). Those four evenings, and the surrounding weekends, are the tightest dates for buses and rooms. Midweek days without an event are usually easier, but still book the mountain buses ahead.
February Snowpack in the mountains often remains from January. Walking can still be icy. The official 2027 light-up dates do not extend into February. February can still be busy. Reserve highway-bus seats and lodging well ahead.
March Early March can still have roadside snow in Takayama and Shirakawa-go, with slush on sunny afternoons. Late March is often the turn toward spring walking weather. Demand can rise again around the late-March holiday period. Recheck bus reservations; winter still delays mountain roads on some days.

January and the 2027 light-up

If the light-up is the reason for your dates, start with the official page: the 41st Shirakawa-go Light-Up Event is on 11 January 2027 (Monday, public holiday), 17 January, 24 January, and 31 January, each 17:30–19:30. The official Shirakawa-go Tourism Association notice, checked 25 November 2026, says the event is completely reservation-only, uses an admission-ticket system, and does not sell tickets on the day. Without a ticket you will be turned away. Ordinary Nohi bus tickets are not entry tickets. Booking steps, parking rules, and daytime restrictions on event dates are in the Shirakawa-go light-up 2027 guide.

For a daytime winter visit that is not tied to those four evenings, use the Takayama in winter and Takayama to Shirakawa-go bus guides instead.

Which direction should you go?

The route is an open-jaw corridor between the Tokaido Shinkansen at Nagoya and the Hokuriku Shinkansen at Kanazawa. Most travelers do not need to backtrack.

South to north: Nagoya → Takayama → Shirakawa-go → Kanazawa

This is the usual direction if you arrive at Chubu Centrair (NGO) or come from Tokyo, Kyoto, or Osaka on the Tokaido Shinkansen. You climb into the mountains, then descend to Kanazawa. From Kanazawa you can continue toward Tokyo on the Hokuriku Shinkansen — JR West’s official Hokuriku Shinkansen page, checked 25 November 2026, lists a Kanazawa–Tokyo travel time of 2 hours 28 minutes; check the day’s Kagayaki or Hakutaka timetable — or transfer at Tsuruga for conventional limited expresses toward Kyoto and Osaka. If you fly into Nagoya, see Centrair to Takayama and Centrair to Nagoya.

North to south: Kanazawa → Shirakawa-go → Takayama → Nagoya

Reverse the loop if you start in Tokyo and ride the Hokuriku Shinkansen to Kanazawa first. Finish in Nagoya for a Tokaido Shinkansen exit toward Kyoto, Osaka, or farther west. I do not recommend a same-day Kanazawa–Shirakawa-go–Kanazawa out-and-back as the default; the village visit works better as a one-way stop between Kanazawa and Takayama. See Kanazawa to Shirakawa-go.

How many days? 4-day and 5-day pacing

Trying to cover all four places in two or three days usually means rushed transfers and little time on the ground. A 4-night / 5-day outline is the more comfortable default. A 3-night / 4-day outline works if your dates are tight. These are planning sketches, not timed itineraries.

Day 5-day pace 4-day pace
Day 1 Arrive in Nagoya; train or reserved highway bus to Takayama. Afternoon in the Sanmachi streets. Overnight in Takayama. Leave Nagoya early for Takayama. Old town and breweries. Overnight in Takayama.
Day 2 Full day in Takayama (morning market, Takayama Jinya, Hida Folk Village if you want a longer walk). Overnight in Takayama. Morning in Takayama, midday bus to Shirakawa-go (store luggage at the bus terminal). Afternoon bus to Kanazawa. Overnight in Kanazawa.
Day 3 Morning bus to Shirakawa-go, a few hours in the village, afternoon bus to Kanazawa. Overnight in Kanazawa. Full day in Kanazawa (Kenroku-en, Omicho Market, Higashi Chaya). Overnight in Kanazawa.
Day 4 Full day in Kanazawa (Kenroku-en, the castle park, Nagamachi). Overnight in Kanazawa. Morning in Kanazawa; afternoon Shinkansen or limited-express departure.
Day 5 Morning at Omicho Market or a leftover district; afternoon onward to Tokyo or Kansai. —

Where to sleep

  • Nagoya (0–1 night). Treat it as a transit hub unless you have a late arrival, an early flight, or a specific reason to stay. Ghibli Park, if you add it, is a separate day from this mountain loop; see Ghibli Park from Nagoya.
  • Takayama (two nights on the 5-day outline). Two nights give you a full day without packing twice. See Takayama in winter and the Takayama 2-day itinerary.
  • Shirakawa-go (a half-day stop, or one night). Most people visit as a luggage-in-storage stop between Takayama and Kanazawa. An overnight in a gassho-style inn is a different trip: rooms are limited, and on light-up dates official lodging is tightly controlled. Book through the official Shirakawa-go Tourism Association lodging pages, and book early. I am not stating a fixed number of months, because I did not find a single official lead-time that covers every inn.
  • Kanazawa (two nights on the 5-day outline). Two nights are enough for the main gardens, market, and historic districts at a winter walking pace. See the Kanazawa 2-day itinerary, one-day itinerary, and Kenroku-en.

Transport and the area pass

Winter travel here is a mix of JR trains and regional highway buses. Official operator pages first; partner booking sites are optional.

1. Nagoya to Takayama (train or bus)

JR Limited Express Hida runs directly from Nagoya Station to Takayama Station (some trains continue to Toyama). Look up the day’s departures, travel time, and fare on official JR Central pages — I am not copying a timetable here, because times change. From 25 December 2026 to 5 January 2027, the official Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass page says all Hida seats are reserved only; obtain a reserved-seat ticket before you board.

The reserved highway bus, jointly run by Nohi Bus, Meitetsu Bus, and JR Tokai Bus, lists a required time of 2 hours 40 minutes on Nohi’s official English Takayama–Nagoya Line page, checked 25 November 2026. All seats are reserved. Adult one-way fares on that page are 3,600 yen, or 3,300 yen with the web discount when you reserve and pay by card on highwaybus.com. Reservations open one month before travel. Compare totals, including any booking fee, if you use a third-party seller.

2. Takayama ↔ Shirakawa-go ↔ Kanazawa (highway bus only)

There is no train station in Shirakawa-go. Public access is by highway bus.

Nohi’s official English Takayama–Shirakawa-go–Kanazawa/Toyama/Takaoka page, checked 25 November 2026, says the ride from Takayama Nohi Bus Center to Shirakawa-go takes about 50 minutes, with some services on a reservation basis, and that after a village visit you can continue by bus to Kanazawa, Toyama, or Takaoka. Many departures on the official timetable are marked reserved (R). The same page says fares and timetables will be revised starting 1 December 2026 — recheck before you travel, especially for December onwards. Use the official timetable for Kanazawa running times rather than a single number copied here.

3. The Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass

The official seller page at touristpass.jp, checked 25 November 2026, lists 19,800 yen for adults and 9,900 yen for children aged 6–11, valid for five consecutive days. Reserved seats on JR limited expresses and the Hokuriku Shinkansen are free of charge up to six times. It is not the nationwide Japan Rail Pass.

Official coverage, in brief:

  • JR limited express, express, rapid, and ordinary trains between Nagoya and Toyama (via the Tokaido and Takayama lines), including Limited Express Hida.
  • JR trains between Tsuruga, Kyoto, and stations in the Osaka city area, and between the Osaka city area and Kansai Airport. This is the official Kansai connection after the Hokuriku Shinkansen extension to Tsuruga: conventional limited expresses on the Tsuruga–Kyoto–Osaka corridor, then the Hokuriku Shinkansen beyond Tsuruga. Recheck the day’s Thunderbird, Shirasagi, and connecting Shinkansen (including Tsurugi) on official JR West pages.
  • Hokuriku Shinkansen ordinary cars between Toyama and Tsuruga — not east of Toyama, and not the Tokaido Shinkansen between Kyoto and Shin-Osaka.
  • Listed Nohi / Hokutetsu / Meitetsu / Toyama Chitetsu (and related) buses on the Takayama–Shirakawa-go–Kanazawa/Toyama corridors, with some exceptions; reservations required. This is not the Nagoya–Takayama highway bus. Do not assume that highway line is included; the official map lists the mountain and Hokuriku bus corridors, not Nagoya–Takayama by bus.

The pass tends to pay off if you are coming from Osaka or Kyoto, riding Hida, using the reserved Shirakawa-go buses, and returning to Kansai within five consecutive days. Individual tickets often make more sense on a one-way Nagoya-to-Tokyo itinerary via Kanazawa, because the pass does not cover the Hokuriku Shinkansen east of Toyama (so Kanazawa–Tokyo is not included). Fare math and caveats: Takayama–Hokuriku Area Pass.

Winter practicalities

Footwear and layers

  • Waterproof boots with a real tread matter more than sneakers. Packed snow in Takayama and Shirakawa-go often turns icy by late afternoon; Kanazawa can be wet slush or cold rain.
  • Compact slip-on ice cleats are useful on packed paths, including the walk up to the Shirakawa-go viewpoint when it is open.
  • Trains, buses, and indoor restaurants are often very warm. Dress in layers you can peel off.

Luggage

Large suitcases are awkward on snowy sidewalks and in limited bus luggage bays. Japan’s luggage-forwarding services (takkyubin, such as Yamato Transport) can send a big bag from a Nagoya hotel toward a Kanazawa hotel — confirm the current cut-off and whether delivery is next-day for your date. Travel the mountain segment with a smaller bag. If you stop in Shirakawa-go between buses, coin lockers and staffed storage at the bus terminal can fill on busy mornings.

Leave a weather buffer

Heavy snow can slow the Tokai–Hokuriku Expressway between Takayama and Kanazawa. Avoid booking a tight, non-refundable flight or a last-train connection immediately after a mountain bus. A free afternoon in Kanazawa, or an overnight there before you leave the region, keeps a delay from becoming a missed departure.

FAQ

When is the Shirakawa-go winter light-up in 2027?
The official Shirakawa-go Tourism Association page for the 41st event lists four dates: 11 January 2027 (Monday, public holiday), 17 January, 24 January, and 31 January, each 17:30–19:30. It is completely reservation-only. Tickets are not sold on the day. Recheck the official page before you travel.
How much is the Takayama–Hokuriku Area Tourist Pass?
The official seller page, checked 25 November 2026, lists 19,800 yen for adults and 9,900 yen for children aged 6–11, valid for five consecutive days. It covers listed JR trains, the Hokuriku Shinkansen between Toyama and Tsuruga, and listed buses on the Takayama–Shirakawa-go–Kanazawa/Toyama corridors. It does not cover the Tokaido Shinkansen or the Hokuriku Shinkansen east of Toyama. Recheck before you buy.
How do I get from Takayama to Shirakawa-go and on to Kanazawa?
There is no train station in the village. Nohi’s official English page, checked 25 November 2026, says the bus from Takayama Nohi Bus Center takes about 50 minutes, with some services on a reservation basis, and that through buses continue to Kanazawa, Toyama, or Takaoka. Fares and timetables are due to change on 1 December 2026. Recheck the official timetable and reserve where required.
