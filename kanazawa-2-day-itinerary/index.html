Two days in Kanazawa is a better fit than trying to add Shirakawa-go as a same-day return. Use the first day for Kenrokuen, the adjoining castle park, and Higashi Chaya, as laid out in the one-day itinerary. Keep the second day slower, or start a one-way journey toward the village and Takayama. Official hours and tickets first; recheck them on the day.

Day 1: garden, castle park, Higashi Chaya

  1. Morning at Kenrokuen. Official Japanese and English guidance (rechecked 26 November 2026) lists paid hours as 7:00–18:00 from 1 March through 15 October (last entry 17:30), and 8:00–17:00 from 16 October through the end of February (last entry 16:30). Individual admission is 320 yen for adults 18 and over and 100 yen for children aged 6–17 (official Japanese wording is ages 6 to under 18). People aged 65 and over enter free with proof of age (the English page mentions a passport). There is also a free early-entry window; only the Renchi-mon and Zuishinzaka gates are open then, and you must leave 15 minutes before paid hours begin. From 1 November through the end of February that window is 6:00–7:45, last entry 7:30.
  2. Walk the adjoining Kanazawa Castle Park. Official combined guidance says the park grounds are free; the restored turret and warehouse buildings have their own tickets and hours. Recheck those building hours on the official page if you want to go inside.
  3. Afternoon in Higashi Chaya District, Kanazawa’s largest historic geisha district, as described by the official Visit Kanazawa guide. Individual teahouses and shops set their own hours — I am not copying café times I have not confirmed on official venue pages.

A “Kenrokuen +1” ticket, listed at 500 yen on the official English guidance page, covers the garden plus one more cultural facility in the city. Confirm what it includes at the window if you want to use it across the two days.

Day 2: stay in the city, or leave one way

A second full day is for a slower walk, a second official cultural facility on the Kenrokuen +1 ticket, or another neighborhood listed on official Visit Kanazawa pages. I am not inventing museum hours here; check the official site for any venue you add.

If you are continuing the loop, use Day 2 to start a one-way trip toward Shirakawa-go and Takayama on the official Nohi / Hokutetsu highway bus. Book the reservation-marked services on Japan Bus Online. That is not a same-day return. See Kanazawa to Shirakawa-go and the Takayama 2-day itinerary.

I do not recommend going to Shirakawa-go and back to Kanazawa in the same day. The highway bus exists, but a short stop between two journeys is a poor fit with how the village asks visitors to behave.

Frequently asked questions

Is two days in Kanazawa enough?
Yes, for Kenrokuen, the castle park, Higashi Chaya, and a slower second day or a one-way departure toward Shirakawa-go and Takayama. Official garden hours are shorter in winter, so start with those times.
Should I add a same-day Shirakawa-go return on one of the two days?
I do not recommend it. Use the highway bus as a one-way link if you are continuing the loop, or stay overnight. A short stop between two journeys is a poor fit with how the village asks visitors to behave.
How much is Kenrokuen?
The official guidance pages list individual admission at 320 yen for adults (18+) and 100 yen for children aged 6–17. People aged 65 and over enter free with proof of age. A Kenrokuen +1 ticket is listed at 500 yen. Recheck before you go.
Can I pay at Kenrokuen by card?
The official Japanese guidance page, checked 26 November 2026, lists cash, several credit cards, and major Japanese e-money brands. The official English guidance page still says cash payments only. Confirm at the window rather than relying on one language version.
